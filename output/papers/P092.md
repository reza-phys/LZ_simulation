# P092: Sidereal and diurnal effects on the high-velocity tail: is there any time-of-day information in a δ ≈ 300–385 keV event?
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-16
- Primary arXiv category: hep-ph (cross-list astro-ph.GA)
- Author profile: dark-matter phenomenology group
- Category (from your taxonomy): IDM
- Builds on / responds to: LZ arXiv:2609.02823; P006, P018, P034, P042, P055, P067

**Abstract.** Near the kinematic edge the inelastic rate is a steep function of the lab speed (P018: 0.82 keV per km/s in δ_max), so we ask whether Earth's rotation imprints a time-of-day signature on LZ's 16 June 21:22:39 UTC event. SURF's rotation speed of 0.332 km/s modulates |v_lab| by ±0.240 km/s over the sidereal day, δ_max by ±0.20 keV and the O₁ rate by 0.6–5.3 %. The event fell 0.073 km/s below the daily mean (LR 0.98–1.00): no information.

**Question and approach.** Does the event's hour carry likelihood information under inelastic DM; how large is the sidereal modulation of δ_max(248 keV) and of the in-ROI rate at δ = 300/350/366/380/385 keV (1 TeV, O₁ isoscalar); how many events would establish it against a time-flat population, compared with P034's annual test; what would a directional detector at SURF have seen; do gravitational focusing or Earth shadowing matter? v_lab(t) = v_⊙ + v_orb(t) + v_rot(t) uses the Baxter-2021 solar motion, astropy's built-in ephemeris and an analytic GMST rotation term at latitude 44.352°. For O₁, dR/dE = Σ_A C_A(E) η(v_min,A): C_A from WimPyDD per isotope (one fast unit stream), η the analytic truncated Maxwellian at the exact |v_lab(t)|, P034's Fig. S2 efficiency.

**Results.** *Frames.* v_rot = 0.332 km/s projected on the apex gives |v_lab| = 265.814 ± 0.240 km/s, maximal at 04:36 UTC; at 21:22:39 UTC, 1.2 h before lower culmination (alt −0.5°; P067), |v_lab| = 265.741 km/s (−0.073) and δ_max(248 keV, 1 TeV) = 387.13 keV, daily range 0.39 keV. The circular-orbit frames of wimprates, WimPyDD and P055 (29.79 km/s; true June speed 29.33) overstate |v_lab| by 0.16 km/s. *Rates* (Table) peak at LST 15.3 h; dlnR/dv saturates at 0.22 per km/s because the 270 keV roll-off, not δ_max, bounds the window; N_3σ is 1240–4750 times P034's annual.

*Direction.* The apex swings from the northern horizon (22:33 UTC) to 89.4° altitude (10:35 UTC): the event recoil was horizontal, SSE; six hours earlier, straight down. *Gravity, shadow.* Earth's potential blueshifts tail particles by a constant 0.08 km/s; Earth focusing is (v_esc/v)² ≈ 2×10⁻⁴, solar focusing changes 3×10⁻⁷ daily. The inelastic channel is kinematically closed in Earth nuclei (Fe ceiling 181 keV); an elastic admixture (λ = 4400 km·10⁻³⁸ cm²/σ_el, paths 1.5–463 km) shadows < 10⁻⁴ only for σ_el < 9.5×10⁻⁴² cm², < 10⁻⁸ at LZ's elastic limit.

**Caveats.** O₁ isoscalar at 1 TeV with P034's efficiency shape. Amplitudes scale with dlnR/dv, which the halo tail sets (v_esc = 528/560 km/s: a₁(366) = 5.4/2.0 %) and which grows in winter (1 Dec: 4.3/6.1 % at 350/366 keV; 57 % at 380 keV, where the rate is ~10⁻⁴ of June's). The Sun's potential adds a constant +1 km/s blueshift (+1 keV in δ_max) ignored by every corpus halo. Earth composition, SURF coordinates and LZ's elastic limit are recalled.

**Conclusion.** There is no time-of-day information in the LZ event: LR = 0.98–1.00 for every δ, and a diurnal test needs thousands of times more events than P034's annual one. The actionable by-product is kinematic: orbital eccentricity lowers |v_lab| on 16 June by 0.16 km/s and δ_max by 0.13 keV.
