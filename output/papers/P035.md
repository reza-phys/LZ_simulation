# P035: Recasting XENONnT and PandaX-4T to inelastic dark matter: the exclusion they could publish tomorrow
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-09
- Primary arXiv category: hep-ph
- Author profile: recasting phenomenologists
- Category (from your taxonomy): XEXP
- Builds on / responds to: LZ arXiv:2609.02823 (Figs. 6, S2, S7; O₁-as-cross-section supplement); P005 (competing: counts vs limits), P007, P015, P021

**Abstract.** Using xenon-target universality we compute the inelastic-O₁ limits XENONnT (3.1 t·yr) and PandaX-4T (1.54 t·yr) would set by extending their analyses to 270–300 keV. Zero events cut LZ's two-sided 90% interval by 15% (XENONnT) or 27% (combined) in log-coupling at every δ, never reaching LZ's best fit (1.63 expected, P(0) = 20%). One XENONnT event would give 4.6–5.2σ combined and, through its energy, the first constraint on δ. XENON1T 2018 lies 11–10⁵× above LZ's upper edge.

**Question and approach.** What formal limit or discovery follows if XENONnT and PandaX-4T open a high-energy NR window, and does the existing XENON1T inelastic limit (LZ Fig. S7, ending at δ = 231 keV) already bite? WimPyDD O₁ˢ spectra (m = 400/1000/4000 GeV, δ = 100 keV to the kinematic ceiling; Sun-frame halo, which P021 showed reproduces LZ's Fig. 6; 12-day annual bracket) are folded with LZ-like efficiency (0.96, 50% at 269.9 keV), a variant (0.85, 50% at 300 keV) and hard edges 150–300 keV. Poisson and Feldman–Cousins 90% limits for 0/1 events with LZ-like backgrounds (2×10⁻⁴–0.0106 per 2.84 t·yr, exposure-scaled); σ_SI = κμ_N²/(πm_v⁴). A joint observed-energy likelihood treats LZ's event plus one XENONnT event.

**Results.** Our LZ normalisation matches P021's Sun-frame scan to 0.3–3% (δ ≤ 350 keV). With LZ-like efficiency the counts at LZ's one-event coupling are exposure ratios, 1.09 + 0.54 = 1.63, for every δ and mass; the 300 keV-edge variant gives 1.47/1.65/2.89/15.2 at δ = 250/350/366/380 keV. Backgrounds are negligible (UL₉₀ = 2.30 and 3.89 for 0 and 1 events; FC agrees to 5%). Zero-event limits at 1 TeV (XENONnT/combined): 6.3/4.2×10⁻⁴³ cm² (δ = 250), 5.0/3.3×10⁻⁴² (300), 4.0/2.7×10⁻⁴⁰ (350 keV), 2.3–6× below LZ's upper edge, yet only κ < 2.11 or 1.41 κ̂_LZ against an interval spanning 0.1–3.7 κ̂: the upper 15%/27% in ln κ is removed (59%/89% at 370/380 keV with the 300 keV edge); LZ's local Z falls 3.59 → 3.30σ. Absolute limits above 360 keV carry a ×1.7–10 halo-tail systematic. *Discovery:* one XENONnT event in 200–270 keV is 3.1–3.5σ alone, 4.6–5.2σ combined; E₂ = 200/230/260 keV gives joint 68% δ = 340–370/360–375/375–385 keV, κ̂ = 0.04/5.8/42. *ROI edge:* 200 keV captures 80–90% of the δ ≤ 300 keV signal but nothing at 380 keV; 300 keV yields 14.9 events there. *XENON1T 2018:* 10.8/26/385/1.2×10⁵ × LZ's upper edge at δ = 100/150/200/231 keV; a naive recast reproduces its δ-shape to ×0.4–1 and its end at 220–230 keV, where E₋(δ) = 44–50 keV leaves the ROI.

**Caveats.** High-energy acceptances of the other TPCs are assumed (counts ∝ plateau); their high-S1 backgrounds are unmodelled beyond LZ-like scaling; the joint fit is a 1D observed-energy proxy with asymptotic Z; LZ edges and the XENON1T curve are digitised (±5%), the latter a PICO recast with unstated assumptions; halo tail ×10 above 360 keV.

**Conclusion.** A 270 keV extension of the existing XENONnT and PandaX-4T data cannot exclude LZ's best fit but would trim the upper quarter of its interval; a single event there would be 5σ-class and, by its energy, would fix δ to ±15 keV. Nothing published constrains LZ's interval.
