# P052: Reproducing LZ's 3.4σ local significance from the published summaries: a three-sample two-dimensional toy likelihood
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-11
- Primary arXiv category: physics.data-an (cross-list hep-ex)
- Author profile: reproducibility-minded statisticians
- Category (from your taxonomy): STAT
- Builds on / responds to: LZ arXiv:2609.02823 (Eq. S1, Tables I/S1/S2/S6/S7, Fig. 5); P016, P021, P008; P004, P022; P009, P024, P038

**Abstract.** We rebuild LZ's three-sample extended likelihood in (S1c, d) from published summaries only, with no anchoring to LZ's significances. Background cells come from the digitised Fig. 5 panels, constraints from Tables I/S1/S2, signals from WimPyDD spectra mapped with the tuned NEST response. Asymptotic Z reproduces 59 table entries to rms 0.42σ (mean −0.22σ); toys give L10 (1 TeV) 3.0–3.2σ against LZ's 3.4σ. The residual sits in unpublished S1c structure of the 250–500 phd background.

**Question and approach.** Can Tables S6/S7 be reproduced from summaries, and which ingredient drives residuals? Cells: S1c < 250 phd (one bin), then 25 phd bins to 600 phd, × 0.5σ bins in d ∈ [−8, 8]. Components: ER bulk/tail, accidentals, MSSI (λ_MSSI), atm-ν+⁸B, detector neutrons (unconstrained; λ_PN, λ_DN), detector ERs/¹²⁷Xe (λ_PG); prompt/delayed samples as Poisson totals; signal split 1 : 10⁻⁴ : 3.1×10⁻². Data: Fig. 5 histograms plus the event at (540.1 phd, d = −1.54). Signal S1c from Nq on LZ's contour scale (P009); d-PDF either N(0,1) or the digitised Fig. 5 L10 curve; resolution 4.9 % (NEST) or 9.3 % (quoted ±23 keV). q0 with ŝ ≥ 0, profiled with iminuit; ≤ 2×10⁴ background-only toys per configuration.

**Results.** The digitised L10 curve sums to 1.12 events, split 0.21/0.59/0.21; with LZ's Fig. 1 spectrum our response gives 0.20/0.59/0.21. Its low-d tail is heavier than N(0,1): 0.070 versus 0.044 in the event's bin (Fig. 4).

*Reproduction (asymptotic, 59 models).* NEST-only Gaussian model: rms 0.68σ, mean −0.48σ; with the Fig. 5 d-shape 0.52/−0.28; with the 23 keV resolution too 0.42/−0.22 (Fig. 1). Pattern reproduced: 0 for isoscalar O1, 1.32 for isovector O1 (LZ 1.3), 2.35 for O4 (2.7), 3.11 for L10 (3.4), 3.04 for O1s δ=350 keV (3.3), isovector inelastic 3.4–3.5 (3.4). Isoscalar inelastic O1 stays 0.5σ low even with LZ's Fig. 1 δ=300 spectrum (2.6σ): the deficit is not spectral.

*L10 fit.* ŝ = 1.18 (+1.60 −0.83) events (LZ 1.0 +1.4 −0.7); pulls ≤ 0.37σ; likelihood ratio at the event 265 per signal event.

*Toys.* Z_toy = 3.03 [2.98, 3.10] (18,559 toys), O1s δ350 2.98, O4 2.55, O1v 1.44, O1s 0. P(q0>0) = 0.03, not Chernoff's 0.5, yet the tail crosses ½χ²₁ near q0 ≈ 9–10, so |Z_toy − Z_asym| ≤ 0.2σ (Fig. 2).

*Ingredients (L10).* 2D→1D −0.6σ; background at the event ×2 −0.2σ; signal d-tail +0.16σ; toys ±0.2σ; nuisance profiling 0.03σ; veto samples 0.00σ. S1c > 500 phd alone gives p0 = 2.4×10⁻⁴ (3.5σ); the modelled 350–500 phd band core adds 3.8×10⁻⁴, lowering Z to 3.1σ.

**Caveats.** Histograms replace the event list; plateau counts were rescaled to 1710. Within-panel S1c shapes for 250–500 phd are modelled. The Fig. 5 d-shape is one best-fit curve applied to all models. The 9.3 % resolution conflicts with the Fig. S2 roll-off (P009) and is a bracketing variant. Toy p-values carry ±25–40 % statistical error.

**Conclusion.** LZ's local significances follow from its published summaries to 0.4σ rms without anchoring, with the 3.4σ reproduced as 3.0–3.2σ; the two-dimensional band variable, not the nuisances, veto samples or toy calibration, carries the result. Closing the last 0.3σ needs the background's S1c dependence inside 250–500 phd (DR-002).
