# P034: Annual modulation as the decisive test for inelastic dark matter near the kinematic edge
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-09
- Primary arXiv category: hep-ph (cross-list astro-ph.CO)
- Author profile: direct-detection statisticians and phenomenologists
- Category (from your taxonomy): PROJ
- Builds on / responds to: LZ arXiv:2609.02823; refines P006 and P020; uses P002, P007, P018, P021, P028

**Abstract.** If LZ's 248 keV event is inelastic dark matter near the kinematic edge, its successors must cluster around 1 June. We compute exact exposure requirements for establishing that time dependence (O₁, 1 TeV, δ = 300–380 keV, normalised to one event per 2.84 t·yr). Against a time-flat population of equal rate, 3σ needs 5–97 events (15–274 t·yr); LZ reaches it only for δ ≳ 366 keV, around 2029–2030. Against the modelled background, timing shortens discovery by at most 36%.

**Question and approach.** What exposure establishes the time dependence of a high-energy NR population at 3σ/5σ, (a) against a time-flat signal of equal rate and (b) against background only, for three estimators, and how do the halo tail, the efficiency roll-off and livetime gaps change it? Time PDFs p(t|δ) come from WimPyDD shell-model kernels times the Baxter-2021 Earth-frame halo on 36 days (periodic spline; 73-day check 10⁻⁴), with the Fig. S2 efficiency. Statistics: (i) known-phase cosine amplitude, (ii) unbinned likelihood ratio Σln[p(t_i)/p_flat], (iii) binomial summer-window count. Median significance from the exact N-fold distribution of each per-event statistic (FFT convolution; zero-rate seasons treated as absorbing), cross-checked with 10⁴ toys. Exposure E = N·2.84 t·yr/μ, μ = 1.0 (band 0.30–2.36).

**Results.** Fundamental amplitudes a₁ = 0.43/0.76/1.18/1.52/1.66 at δ = 300/330/350/366/380 keV (a₁ > 1: non-sinusoidal, cf. P028); harmonics carry 0–29% of the variance; at 380 keV the rate vanishes 15% of the year. *Versus a flat population* the likelihood ratio needs N_3σ = 96.5/29.4/11.5/6.5/5.3 and N_5σ = 268/82/32/17.6/14.1 events, i.e. E_3σ = 274/84/33/19/15, E_5σ = 762/232/91/50/40 t·yr at the best fit (×0.42–3.3 over the 68% band); May–August counting needs 194/60/26/14/12 events. A known-phase cosine test loses ≤ 3% (the rule Z = a₁√(N/2) errs by ≤ 25% at small N); P006's 107/18 (δ = 300/350) become 97/12 with the corrected halo grid. LZ reaches 3σ in 2029/2030/2035 for δ = 380/366/350 keV; at 12.9 t·yr its median is 0.7–2.8σ (1.0–4.3σ at the upper rate); a 60 t detector needs 0.3–5.7 yr. *Versus background*, counting alone discovers at 10.6 t·yr (four events, panel background) or 5.0 t·yr (two, neighbourhood); timing lowers only the former, to 7.8–10.6 t·yr (gain ≤ 1.36). *Degradations*: v_esc = 528/560 km/s gives N_3σ(350) = 7.0/20.6; a 21-day gap shifts N_3σ by −8 to +10%. The 270 keV roll-off *enhances* modulation (winter recoils sit at E* ≈ δμ/m_N ≈ 330 keV): removing it lowers a₁(366) to 1.19 but raises the rate ×4.9, so a 400 keV ROI reaches 3σ ×2.8 (366) to ×21 (380 keV) sooner.

**Caveats.** Uniform livetime over whole years; simple-vs-simple tests with δ and phase fixed (fitting them costs one to two units of −2lnL); O₁ isoscalar shapes at one mass (P002: mass-insensitive for δ ≥ 350 keV); the Fig. S2 roll-off controls the δ ≥ 366 keV normalisation (P020); the 60 t figure assumes 80% live fraction and LZ-like acceptance; halo variants are truncated Maxwellians, P018's non-Maxwellian tails enter only through rates.

**Conclusion.** Timing is decisive only against a time-flat alternative, where it is the sole discriminant: 5–12 events (15–33 t·yr) for δ ≥ 350 keV, beyond LZ's likely lifetime except at δ ≳ 366 keV; a 60 t detector or an ROI extended past 300 keV settles it within two years.
