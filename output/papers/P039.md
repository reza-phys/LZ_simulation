# P039: Neutron-star kinetic heating by TeV inelastic dark matter: does the LZ-fit cross-section light up old neutron stars?
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-09
- Primary arXiv category: astro-ph.HE (cross-list hep-ph)
- Author profile: compact-object astrophysicists
- Category (from your taxonomy): COMP
- Builds on / responds to: LZ arXiv:2609.02823; P007 (Higgsino), P011 (dark photon), P021 (σ_n(δ) fit), P014/P026 (χ₂ lifetimes), P002

**Abstract.** Inelastic dark matter with δ ≈ 300–380 keV barely scatters in the halo but scatters freely in a neutron star, where infalling particles bring 243 MeV per nucleon. Every LZ-fit cross-section exceeds the geometric capture threshold by 30–3×10⁷, so an old isolated neutron star is heated to T_∞ ≈ 1600 K (2400 K with annihilation), model-independently. White dwarfs scatter it but stay cold; the Sun captures it only on core iron, where a Higgsino annihilates at ~10²² s⁻¹.

**Question and approach.** Is capture saturated, what temperature and near-infrared flux follow, what would a 2000 K star imply, and what do white dwarfs and the Sun add? We take M = 1.5 M_⊙, R = 12 km with the Schwarzschild factor B = 0.631 (v_esc = 0.61c, capture area πR²/B, efficiency 1−√B = 0.206), the Baxter halo through lzcommon (⟨1/v⟩⁻¹ = 288 km/s), ρ_χ = 0.3–0.4 GeV cm⁻³, a chord-averaged capture probability f(σ/σ_crit), blackbody fluxes against a recalled NIRCam sensitivity, Helm-weighted capture windows on C/O in a 1 M_⊙ white dwarf, and WimPyC's solar profile for δ-dependent capture on ⁵⁶Fe/⁵⁸Ni, cross-checked by our own Gould integral to 0.1–2 %.

**Results.** σ_crit,n = πR²/N_n = 2.5×10⁻⁴⁵ cm² (3.6×10⁻⁴⁴ proton-only); δ = 380 keV is 0.2 % of the available 243 MeV. Higgsino (σ/σ_crit = 2.9×10⁶), dark photon (33–6×10⁴) and P021 fits (900–3×10⁷) are saturated; χ₂ re-scatters within 0.06 cm–480 m, so its decay is irrelevant. Ṅm_χ = ρπR_∞²[B⟨v⟩ + v_esc²⟨1/v⟩] = 59 g s⁻¹ (ρ_χ = 0.4), giving

| ρ_χ | T_∞ kinetic | + annihilation |
|---|---|---|
| 0.3 | 1497 K | 2223 K |
| 0.4 | 1609 K | 2389 K |

At 10 pc the star gives 0.43 nJy (AB 32.3) at 2 μm, needing ~10⁶ s of NIRCam (5×10⁴ s with annihilation). A 2000 K star needs ρ_χ ≈ 0.95 (kinetic) or 0.20 GeV cm⁻³ (annihilation); a cold (< 1400 K) old star excludes all fits. White dwarfs: δ_max = 2.0–6.7 MeV on C/O and σ_crit per nucleon 1.2–5×10⁻⁴¹ cm², so δ ≥ 350 keV fits saturate yet heat it only to 50 K (425 K with annihilation; 3000 K in M4 at 10³ GeV cm⁻³, 0.3× its faintest white dwarf). Sun: only core Fe/Ni (δ_max = 542/561 keV) can scatter; they capture the δ = 366 keV Higgsino at C = 1.3×10²³ s⁻¹, Γ_A = 6.5×10²² s⁻¹ in equilibrium (1.1×10²² if thermalisation stalls), 10²–10³ above the 10²⁰ s⁻¹ IceCube scale; dark photons: 5×10¹⁹–2×10²² s⁻¹.

**Caveats.** Uniform density and straight chords (harmless at τ̄ ≥ 33); a ×0.03 nucleon form-factor suppression leaves only the δ = 250 keV dark photon partially captured (f = 0.6); a 400 km/s kick lowers T by 8 %; the literature 1700/2500 K, NIRCam sensitivity, neutron-star distances, M4 density and IceCube scale are recalled; ISM accretion (up to 5×10⁸× the DM heating) and internal heat sources confound a positive detection.

**Conclusion.** Neutron-star heating tests the LZ inelastic fits independently of the halo tail and of δ: all give T_∞ ≈ 1600 K. Detection needs an old neutron star within ~10 pc and ~10⁶ s of JWST; a cold one refutes them. P076 must confront the Sun's Higgsino annihilation rate with IceCube.
