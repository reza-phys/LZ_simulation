# P032: Why the isovector O1 gets 1.3σ at δ = 0 while the isoscalar gets none: isospin, the M-response node and what isospin violation can buy

- Primary arXiv category: hep-ph

**Abstract.** LZ's O₁ receives 0σ for isoscalar but 0.8–1.3σ for isovector couplings. With WimPyDD's shell-model M responses we show why: the isovector amplitude ZF_p − NF_n has its second node at 182 keV instead of 267 keV, so at 248 keV both couplings give the same rate at fixed c_p while at low energy the isovector gives ×0.022. Scanning f_n/f_p, no ratio brings N_lo below 6; for inelastic O₁ only pure isovector coupling puts 248 keV at the spectral peak.

**Question and approach.** How does the isovector M response (M⁰¹, M¹¹) produce the Table S6/S7 asymmetry, and how far can isospin violation reduce the low-energy population? The rate is a quadratic form, dR/dE = c⁰²R₀₀ + 2c⁰c¹R₀₁ + c¹²R₁₁ (WimPyDD c⁰ = c_p(1+r), c¹ = c_p(1−r), r = f_n/f_p), so three WimPyDD spectra per mass (400/1000/4000 GeV; annual Baxter halo) give every r exactly (check 3×10⁻¹³). We compute efficiency-weighted rates in 5.4–55, 55–200, 200–270 keV, N_lo, node positions (natural Xe and per isotope), local Z with P016's few-bin likelihood (b_H = 5.7×10⁻⁴), and δ = 300 keV percentiles, including neutron-only and P007's Higgsino (c¹/c⁰ = −1.17, r = −12.8).

**Results.** *Mechanism.* f_n falls faster than f_p (valence neutrons occupy higher orbitals), so ZF_p − NF_n vanishes at 69 and 182 keV against 102 and 267 keV for the isoscalar. At 248 keV the isoscalar sits 19 keV below its node, the isovector at its post-node maximum: the isovector/isoscalar rate at equal c_p is 0.022 (5.4–55 keV) but 0.99 (248 keV).

Our isovector Z is 0.3–0.6σ high; LZ's 1.3σ implies N_lo = 878, 1.8× WimPyDD's — LZ's modified density matrices or two-body currents need only halve W¹¹ near 248 keV.

*Isospin violation.* N_lo(r) is minimal at r = −0.744: 10.4/7.3/6.3 (400/1000/4000 GeV); the isotope spread (Z − 0.7N = +2.2…−3.4) limits the low-energy suppression to 1.1×10⁻³. N_lo ≤ 5 is never reached.

*Inelastic δ = 300 keV.* 248 keV percentile: 99.5 % (r = 1), 97.6 % (0), 79 % (−0.7), 70 % (−1; spectrum peaks at 252 keV), 98.4 % (Higgsino: neutron-like, node 245 keV).

**Caveats.** Stock DMFormFactor-v6 one-body density matrices; LZ's modifications unknown; no two-body currents. ¹³¹Xe's M response has two multipoles (W⁰¹²/W⁰⁰W¹¹ = 0.44), so nodes are minima, not zeros. 1D true-energy proxy with P016's anchored b_H (×2 → O1ᵛ Z 1.25–2.00); LZ's own identical-spectrum entries (L1ᵛ/L5ᵛ/O1ᵛ) scatter 0.3–0.8σ at 400 GeV. N_lo thresholds are P003's recalled criterion.

**Conclusion.** O1ᵛ's 1.3σ is a node effect — the event sits at the isovector maximum and on the isoscalar node — not evidence. No f_n/f_p makes elastic SI compatible with a lone event (N_lo ≥ 6); for inelastic O₁ only pure isovector coupling makes 248 keV typical.
