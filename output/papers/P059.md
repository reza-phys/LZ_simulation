# P059: Archival xenon exposures reinterpreted for the extended window: what LUX, XENON1T, PandaX-II and LZ's own first run already said
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-11
- Primary arXiv category: hep-ex (cross-list hep-ph)
- Author profile: former LUX/XENON analysts
- Category (from your taxonomy): XEXP
- Builds on / responds to: LZ arXiv:2609.02823 (Fig. 6 previous limits, Fig. S7, ROI statement); P005, P035 (xenon universality), P012 (L10 normalisation), P007/P021 (edges), P038 (acceptance)

**Abstract.** Four archival xenon searches predate LZ's result: LZ SR1 (0.90 t·yr, same S1c 3–600 phd window), XENON1T (1.0 t·yr), LUX 2021 (0.09 t·yr) and PandaX-II 2019 (0.15 t·yr). At LZ's best fit they should have contained 0.29–0.34 events, 0.29–0.32 of them in SR1 (P(0) = 0.73); the others contribute ≤ 0.03 because their windows end below the signal. The digitised SR1 limits correspond to 2–3 zero-event counts and sit 6–30× above the new best fit.

**Question and approach.** How many events did the archival exposures owe LZ's fit, are the previous limits in Fig. 6 arithmetically consistent with that, and what does a joint likelihood do? We fold WimPyDD spectra normalised to 1.0 LZ event (P005; own δ = 366 keV spectrum; L10 from P012) with each exposure's recalled acceptance in true recoil energy: SR1 uses LZ's roll-off shifted to 260 keV (g1 = 0.114), XENON1T 4.9–40.9 keV, LUX and PandaX-II hard edges scanned over 100–250 and 50–150 keV. The Fig. 6 violet/red/blue curves are digitised from the vector PDF and converted into implied counts (normalisations validated against P035 and P012 to 0.1 %). A Poisson likelihood combines LZ's one event with archival zeros.

**Results.** *Counts.* SR1 expects 0.308 (L10), 0.318/0.317/0.313 (δ = 250/300/350 keV) and 0.286 (366 keV) events; XENON1T 0.018 (L10) and zero for every inelastic model (onsets ≥ 55 keV); LUX 0–0.011 and PandaX-II 0–0.007. Totals 0.29–0.34, P(0) = 0.71–0.75; with LZ-like acceptance on all 2.14 t·yr, 0.755 events (P(0) = 0.47).
*Consistency.* The SR1 O₁ limit at δ = 250 keV corresponds to 1.96 events in 0.90 t·yr — a zero-event PLR-type limit — and to 3.7 events at 200 keV; at δ ≤ 150 keV the implied 5–9 events show those limits were background-limited. The new best fit lies 6.2× (250 keV) to 30× (100 keV) below the SR1 limit, the new upper edge only 1.6–1.9× (the event lifts it), the median sensitivity 3.2–6.3× (exposure ratio 3.14). For L10 the SR1 limits imply 2.6–3.2 events (m ≥ 200 GeV) in LZ's Fig.-1 normalisation but 0.68–0.83 in the WimPyDD/Anand one — below any 90 % CL construction — so SR1 independently favours P012's normalisation (DR-001).
*Combination (b = 2 × 10⁻⁴).* LZ alone: ŝ = 0.352 events/t·yr, 90 % interval 0.037–1.28, Z = 3.88 (Poisson proxy). Archival zeros with actual ROIs (effective 0.81–0.97 t·yr): ŝ = 0.26–0.27 (×0.75–0.78), interval 0.028–0.96/1.00, one-sided UL 1.01 → 0.76–0.79, Z − 0.07. LZ-like acceptance everywhere: ŝ × 0.57, UL 0.58, Z − 0.15.
*SR1 look-back.* A joint SR1+SR3 fit with zero SR1 events cuts the rate by 24 %; one SR1 NR-band event at S1c 400–600 phd would give ŝ × 1.5 and Z 3.88 → 5.64.

**Caveats.** All archival exposures, edges and plateaus are recalled (SR1 exposure and g1 likely; LUX/PandaX-II edges and "zero SR1 high-energy events" uncertain). Digitisation ±5 %; Sun-frame halo; Z values are asymptotic Poisson proxies, only ΔZ is meaningful.

**Conclusion.** Nothing published before September 2026 constrains LZ's fit: the only archival exposure that could see the signal is LZ's own SR1, which owed 0.3 events and set limits 6–30× above the best fit. A joint SR1+SR3 analysis is the cheapest decisive step: a null trims the rate by a quarter, one event would be 5σ-class.
