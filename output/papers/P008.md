# P008: An independent look-elsewhere estimate for 616 models tested on one event
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-04
- Primary arXiv category: physics.data-an (cross-list hep-ex)
- Author profile: statistical methods group in astroparticle physics
- Category (from your taxonomy): STAT
- Builds on / responds to: LZ arXiv:2609.02823 (Results; Supplement LEE and local-significance sections, Tables I, S6, S7, Fig. 5); P001 (same-day companion)

**Abstract.** LZ reduces a 3.4σ local excess to 2.6σ globally after testing 616 models (293 distinct spectra), implying ≈14 effective trials. With a simplified one-dimensional likelihood, WimPyDD spectra for 456 operator and inelastic models and 300,000 background-only toys we find N_eff = 12.2 ± 0.5 (stat) ± 1.2 (method) at 3.4σ and a global 2.64σ: the reduction follows. N_eff depends on the model space (4–15) and threshold (4.8 at 2σ); Bonferroni (1.3σ) errs by a factor 20.

**Question and approach.** How many independent trials do 293 spectra represent when the data contain one 248 keV event, and how does the global significance depend on what is tested? We build an extended likelihood in observed recoil energy (5.4–270 keV) inside the NR band: background 2.92 events (accidentals 2.7, 15 keV exponential; computed atmospheric-ν CEνNS 0.11; MSSI 0.0049 flat above 50 keV; neutrons 0.05; ⁸B below threshold), 0.0022 above 200 keV; ER leakage neglected (Fig. 5). Signals: O₁, O₃–O₁₅ × isoscalar/isovector × 13 masses, LZ's inelastic O₁/O₄ grid, plus a 10 keV-step δ scan; smeared with σ = 23√(E/248) keV and the LZ efficiency. Profile-likelihood q₀ per model, toy-calibrated local p, min-p over models (150k + 150k toys).

**Results.** Spectral degeneracies match LZ's list (O₄ s/v CDFs differ by 0.5%; 400 vs 1000 GeV by 0.7–5%); 287 of 456 shapes are distinct at 1%. Wilks asymptotics fail in the bulk (P(q₀>0) = 0.18–0.45), so calibration is essential. For the full LZ-like space,

  p_global(p_local = 3.37×10⁻⁴) = (4.1 ± 0.2)×10⁻³ → Z_global = 2.64σ, N_eff = ln(1−p_global)/ln(1−p_local) = 12.2,

against LZ's 4.7×10⁻³, 2.6σ, 13.9. N_eff rises with threshold: 4.8, 6.0, 8.3, 12.2 at 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 3.4σ. By subspace (3.4σ): single model 0.7–1.4; 140 heavy elastic models 4.5 (2.96σ) — all see the same event; 224 light models 3.9 via low-energy count fluctuations; inelastic grid (96) 9.9 (2.71σ), each δ selecting a different resolution-wide window; a continuous δ scan of one operator 1.6 → 9.3 as δ_max goes 100 → 400 keV (+25% beyond LZ's 350 keV cut-off; the 8-point grid gives 4.2); full space plus scan 14.5 (2.58σ). Bonferroni: 1.29σ (293 models). Gross–Vitells (asymptotic Z) tracks the scan's growth but underestimates calibrated N_eff by ~2.5. Robustness: N_eff = 8.7–13.2 for ×3/÷2 high-energy background and 10–20 keV accidentals scale. The data's local Z reproduces Tables S6/S7: ≈0 for m ≤ 50 GeV and isoscalar O₁, 1.5 for isovector O₁, 2.5–3.0 for momentum-suppressed operators above 400 GeV, 3.4–3.8 for inelastic δ ≥ 300 keV; ours run 0.3–0.5σ low (1D background at 248 keV ~6× LZ's).

**Caveats.** One observable instead of {S1c, log S2c}; no nuisance profiling (would shrink the light-model trials, N_eff → ~9–10); low-energy NR-band background is an assumed exponential; calibrated p-values are discrete with three events (single-model N_eff 0.7–1.4 sets a ±1.2 floor); our operator basis is not LZ's Lagrangian list, though it spans more heavy-mass shapes.

**Conclusion.** An independent toy calculation gives N_eff ≈ 12 and 2.6σ global: LZ's correction is right. The trials factor is set by the number of resolution-wide energy windows a lone event can fall into, not by the model count; an extended δ scan would lower the global significance further.
