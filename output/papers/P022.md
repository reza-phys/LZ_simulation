# P022: Accidental coincidences at high S1: could an isolated 540 phd S1 and a 270-electron S2 fake the LZ event?
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-08
- Primary arXiv category: physics.ins-det (cross-list hep-ex)
- Author profile: xenon-TPC analysts specialising in pulse-pairing backgrounds
- Category (from your taxonomy): BKG
- Builds on / responds to: LZ paper (arXiv:2609.02823) accidentals model, Fig. 5, Fig. S3; P004 (method), P009, P010; dossier §5-E

**Abstract.** LZ lists accidental S1–S2 pairings among the dominant backgrounds at its 248 keV candidate but validates the model with unphysical-drift-time (UDT) events that are almost all below 100 phd. Digitising Figs. 5 and S3 we find 1.5×10⁻⁴ accidentals within ±2σ_NR at S1c > 500 phd; a 10 % accidental origin needs a ×710 mismodelling, which LZ's zero post-cut UDT events above 495 phd exclude at 4.3σ. One topology (cathode-emission S2 at long drift) deserves a dedicated check.

**Question and approach.** What does the accidentals model predict at (540 phd, log₁₀S2c = 3.97, drift ≈ 860 μs), which isolated-pulse rates does it imply, can charge-insensitive-region depositions supply them, and what mismodelling would make an accidental likely? We digitise the yellow histogram in all three Fig. 5 panels (P004's tick-calibrated method), the S1c and log₁₀S2c projections, UDT data points and viridis map of Fig. S3a/b (colour bar inverted; 2D bin height fixed by normalisation), and the Fig. 3 drift axis (T_max = 1049 μs, t_ev = 859 μs). The pair rate factorises, N = R_S1 R_S2 T_max T_live f_cuts; NEST-LZ yields set the ER energy of a 500–600 phd S1 and the S1 any real 269-electron cluster must carry.

**Results.** Fig. 5 accidentals sum to 2.47 (Table I fit 2.6): 99.86 % below 250 phd, 6.3×10⁻⁴ above 500 phd; within ±2σ_NR, N_nb = 1.49×10⁻⁴.

Fig. S3: 0.68 % of post-cut isolated S1s lie above 495 phd (2.6 % pre-cut; 22 UDT events vs 14.6); the isolated-S2 spectrum falls ×9 across the ROI, and the S1c 500–600 column puts 2.1 % in ±2σ_NR. S3b predicts 3.7×10⁻⁴ there; Fig. 5 gives 0.41 of it (FV/PDT acceptance).

Rates: R_S1R_S2f_cuts = 1.35×10⁻⁴ Hz². If the UDT window equals T_max, R_S1(500–600)R_S2 = 7×10⁻⁴ Hz²: for R_S2 = 10–100 mHz, 600–6000 such S1s per day versus 1.1/day from β decays in the 0.65 t RFR (84–104 keV_ee), so the source must be detector-γ light beside the bottom PMTs: bottom-heavy S1s disfavoured by LZ's hit-pattern test. A 269-electron bulk cluster carries S1 ≈ 58 (β) or 336 phd (NR), so an S1-less S2 is electrode emission: gate emission fails the width cut; cathode emission (1049 μs) is only 2.4σ_stat wider than a bulk S2 at 859 μs and passes a ±10–20 % tolerance for drift > 850–672 μs, where the event sits.

**Caveats.** The UDT check at S1c > 500 phd is a Poisson zero against 0.016 expected; the exclusion assumes UDT and PDT pairs share cut acceptances at high S1 and that the isolated-S2 shape is not peaked at the NR band. Absolute rates rest on the UDT-window assumption and recalled brackets. Pixel reading: ±0.01–0.02 dex; S3b sums read 9 % above 2.4.

**Conclusion.** The model gives P = 1.5×10⁻⁴ for an accidental at the event's location; the ×700 error needed is excluded at 4σ by LZ's high-S1 UDT zero, though the margin (708 vs 183) is 100× thinner than for wall MSSI. LZ should test the S2 width against a cathode-drift template.
