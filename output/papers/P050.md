# P050: How quickly would a 60-tonne xenon detector confirm or exclude the LZ high-energy hint?
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-10
- Primary arXiv category: hep-ex (cross-list hep-ph)
- Author profile: next-generation xenon detector planners
- Category (from your taxonomy): PROJ
- Builds on / responds to: LZ arXiv:2609.02823 (Table I, Figs. S2/S4); P020, P034, P038, P021, P012, P005/P035, P004/P022/P019, P015

**Abstract.** We project LZ's best-fit L10 and inelastic (δ = 300–380 keV) rates onto the xenon generation now running and a 40–60 t detector with ROIs to 300–600 keV. Size hardly matters: the background above 200 keV is ≲ 10⁻³ events per 2.84 t·yr. A 60 t detector with a 400 keV window reaches 5σ in 8.5 (L10) to 0.13 (δ = 380) t·yr, excludes the lower 90 % edge within a year, and separates L10 from inelastic-366 with 4–6 events.

**Question and approach.** For LZ-like (E50 = 270 keV) and extended (300/400/600 keV) windows, what exposure gives (a) 5σ, (b) zero-event exclusion of the LZ lower edge (0.105–0.38 events per 2.84 t·yr), (c) 3σ L10-vs-inelastic separation, (d) δ to ±10 keV? WimPyDD spectra (1 TeV, annual Baxter halo) are normalised to one LZ-ROI event and folded with erf efficiencies and σ_E = 11√(E/248) keV. NR-band background per 2.84 t·yr (P016/P004/P022/P019/P038): 0.10 (100–125 keV), 0.02 (125–200), 5.7×10⁻⁴ (200–270), plus MSSI 0.003 to 400 keV; MSSI scales as FV surface/volume = 2/R (0.73 → 1.50 m), accidentals and ν per tonne. Binned Asimov Z; CLs exclusion; toy likelihood ratios; Fisher information.

**Results.** A 400 keV edge raises the accepted fraction of the L10/350/366/380 keV spectra from 42/44/17/2.8 % to 54/87/83/83 %, i.e. best-fit rates 0.45/0.69/1.70/10.5 per t·yr (for δ ≥ 366 keV these conflict with LZ's empty 600–1000 phd region, P038). Background above 200 keV per 2.84 t·yr: 6.0×10⁻⁴ (LZ window), 2.7×10⁻³ (400 keV; 1.6×10⁻³ for 60 t); 5σ means 2–4 events, so 40 t and 60 t differ by 1–2 %. E_5σ = 12.3/11.5/8.5/6.8/5.7 t·yr (L10/300/350/366/380) in an LZ-like window and 8.5/9.6/3.6/1.08/0.13 t·yr with a 400 keV window (×4.6 at the lower 68 % rate): at 48 t·yr/yr the 60 t detector needs 64/73/28/8/1 live days. Zero events exclude the 0.105 (0.38) lower edge at 90 % with 62 (17) t·yr in an LZ-like window, 49 (13.6) for L10 and 12.9 (3.6) for δ = 366 keV with 400 keV: one 60 t year. L10 puts 25 % of its events outside the 167–437 keV support of the 366 keV spectrum: 4 events separate them at 3σ if L10 is true, 5–6 if inelastic is true (toys). One event fixes δ to 15–27 keV; ±10 keV needs 2–8 events (0.7–7.6 t·yr), from the onset E₋(δ), not the endpoint (≤ 4 %); v_esc ± 20 km/s shifts δ by ±16 keV (P018). P015's W/Xe ratios need ≈ 4 events per target.

**Caveats.** Asimov Z is asymptotic (exact exposures ≲ 15 % larger); extension backgrounds are P038's interpolation (×2) plus our estimates (×3), immaterial while s ≫ b; the S2c ceiling is assumed to track the NR band; XLZD mass and start, fiducial masses and live fractions are recalled; annual-average halo; one mass per class. P045/P046 not yet available.

**Conclusion.** The hint is exposure-, not background-limited: a 60 t detector settles the best-fit rate within months and the lower edge within a year, but the present generation with a 400 keV ROI can do most of this by 2030. Shape needs 4–6 events; δ is halo-limited beyond ±16 keV.
