# P016: Why does LZ's likelihood still give 2.7σ to spectra with tens of low-energy events? A joint low/high-energy reassessment
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-05
- Primary arXiv category: physics.data-an (cross-list hep-ph)
- Author profile: statistics-minded direct-detection phenomenologists
- Category (from your taxonomy): STAT
- Builds on / responds to: LZ paper arXiv:2609.02823 (Tables I, S6, S7, Fig. 5); P001; P003; P008

**Abstract.** P003 found that elastic O4 (LZ's L15) needs N_lo ≈ 28 events in 5.4–55 keV per 200–270 keV event yet receives 2.7σ. We reproduce LZ's Tables S6/S7 with a few-bin likelihood built from Fig. 5 and show why: the profile fit shrinks the signal to ŝ = 0.06 events at 248 keV (1.6 low-energy companions), and the gain ln(ŝ/b_H) is logarithmic. Local Z is nearly blind to spectral shape; Bayes factors are not (O4: 1.6, L10: 45).

**Question and approach.** How much low-energy signal does LZ's unbinned likelihood tolerate, why do N_lo ≈ 30 spectra keep ~2.7σ, and what survives when the 2024 null is imposed explicitly? We digitise the S1c < 250 phd panel of Fig. 5 (background 58.6 model / 42 observed within ±1.5σ of the NR median; 5.7 / 2 below it) and build an extended Poisson likelihood over its 0.5σ bins, a 125–200 keV bin (b = 0.02, n = 0) and the 200–270 keV bin (n = 1, b_H = 5.7 × 10⁻⁴, anchored so that L10 gives 3.4σ). Signal per unit ŝ: N_lo plus 55–200 keV companions (P003 spectra); ER-leakage scale profiled; Wilks Z checked with toys (≤ 0.12σ).

**Results.** *Calibration* (Z_model/Z_LZ, 1000 GeV): L10 3.40/3.4, O6 3.26/3.1, O10 3.08/3.0, O4 2.70/2.7, O11^v 2.73/2.6, O11^s 1.90/1.7, O1^v 1.65/1.3, O1^s 0.48/0.0; rms 0.21σ.

*Mechanism.* With one low-energy bin (n_L = b_L) the profile maximum satisfies b_L u² = 1 + u, u = ŝN_lo/b_L, so ŝ ≈ √b_L/N_lo and

  Z² ≈ 2[ln(√b_eff/(N_lo b_H)) − ½],  b_eff = 1/Σg_i²/b_i = 22.5.

Companions cost a saturating ½; the single-event gain falls only as ln N_lo. Z drops below 3σ at N_lo = 11, 2σ at 160, 1σ at 940. Every N_lo ≥ 28 model fits 1.6–2.2 low-energy events — the fit's real tolerance — and best-fit O4 predicts the observed event with probability 5.5 % (L10: 39 %).

*Explicit 2024 null* (Gaussian, 90 % UL N_max = 3–20): maximum shift 0.16σ; classes unchanged (≥ 3σ: L10, O6, O15, O5^v, O9, O14, O13^s, O10, all N_lo ≤ 4.9; < 2σ: O11^s, O1). Alone, it keeps every N_lo ≤ 50 model above 2.85σ: a soft constraint is absorbed by shrinking ŝ.

*Bayes factors* (P001 formula, flat s ∈ [0,10]; 176 without low-energy data): L10 45; O6 18; O15^s 18; O5^v 17; O9 13; O14 12; O10 6.3; O4 1.6; O11^v 1.6; O11^s, O1 1.0. With equal priors, 87 % of the posterior mass sits in N_lo ≤ 5 operators, 6 % in N_lo > 20. Scaling the low-energy background ×0.5–3 changes Z by ≤ 0.07σ.

**Caveats.** A 1D projection replaces LZ's 2D PDFs; the S1c < 250 phd panel is a proxy for S1c < 80 phd (halving it changes Z by < 0.03σ). b_H is anchored to LZ's 3.4σ, so only differences are ours. The Lagrangian ↔ operator mapping is P003's (likely); L10's 55–200 keV companions follow O6. The residual +0.5σ for O1^s suggests a larger real deficit at low S1c.

**Conclusion.** LZ's local significances are a statement about one event at b_H ≈ 6 × 10⁻⁴, insensitive (logarithmically) to the spectrum; they do not endorse O1-, O4-, O7-, O11-, O12-type interactions, whose Bayes factors are 1.0–1.6. Evidence, such as it is, belongs to q²-suppressed spin couplings and L10.
