# P006: Sixteenth of June: the event date under inelastic dark matter and under background
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-03
- Primary arXiv category: astro-ph.CO (cross-list hep-ph)
- Author profile: astroparticle theorists working on halo modelling and annual modulation
- Category (from your taxonomy): HALO
- Builds on / responds to: LZ paper arXiv:2609.02823; P000 dossier (§3.3 timing); P001 (Bayes factors)

**Abstract.** LZ's 248 keV event fell on 16 June 2023, fifteen days after the annual-modulation maximum. We compute the likelihood ratio of that date under flat backgrounds and under WimPyDD spectra for elastic and inelastic (δ = 100–380 keV) dark matter over the 220-live-day run. The date multiplies the odds by 1.4 (δ = 300 keV) to 2.1 (350 keV), carries only 0.04–0.3 nats of expected information, and raises δ_max by 11 keV.

**Question and approach.** For one event the date enters only through the normalised time density p(t|M) ∝ R_M(t) L(t), so LR = R_M(t_ev)/⟨R_M⟩_run. We evaluate the in-ROI rate (5.4–270 keV, Fig. S2 efficiency) on 42 days of the year with WimPyDD (Baxter-2021 halo) for O1 elastic (100, 1000 GeV) and O1 inelastic at 1000 GeV, δ = 100–380 keV, with uniform livetime and with three assumed one-week calibration gaps (one at the 8 June AmBe deployment). Information content: E_M[ln LR] = KL(p_M‖p_flat) and its variance; events for 3σ from dates alone, N = 9 Var_flat/(E_M−E_flat)², checked by toys. Three Earth-velocity models agree to 1.9 km/s.

**Results.** The Earth speed peaks on 1–2 June (amplitude 14.5–15.0 km/s); on the event date v_E = 266 km/s, v_max = 810 km/s. ROI modulation fractions: elastic SI 0.7% (100 GeV) and 2.4% (1000 GeV), both peaking in *December* (low-v_min phase reversal); inelastic 10/24/41/90/100% for δ = 100/200/300/350/380 keV (at 380 keV the rate vanishes for ~30% of the year). June/December ratio at δ = 300 keV: 2.34.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| SI elastic | 0.977 | 0.166 | 0.0001 (0.017) | 3×10⁴ |

Flat: P(±30 d) = 0.162; gaps change LR by < 4% (1.42, 2.12, 2.63). Only 32 of 371 run days predict a rate ≥ the 16 June value: p_flat = 0.086 (1.36σ) for any June-peaked model; under δ = 300 keV the observed ln LR = 0.34 is 1.0 sd above expectation. Toys confirm N_3σ within ±0.3σ. P001's B₁₀ = 7–37 becomes 10–52 (δ = 300) or 15–77 (δ = 350 keV). Kinematics: δ_max(248 keV, 1000 GeV) = 387.3 keV on 16 June versus 375.9 (annual mean) and 363.8 keV (December); 248 keV is allowed 144 days per year at δ = 380 keV and never at 390 keV.

**Caveats.** Backgrounds are taken time-flat; a calibration-linked transient (AmBe 8 days earlier) would have a sharper time PDF and is not addressed. The livetime distribution is unpublished; gaps are assumed. Spectra are O1 shapes; dipole-type operators were represented only by an O11 proxy (LR 0.98). Efficiency roll-off read from Fig. S2 by eye.

**Conclusion.** The June date is what inelastic dark matter predicts and modestly favours it—×1.4 for δ = 300 keV, ×2.1 for 350 keV—but it is a 1.4σ, 0.04–0.3 nat observation; ~20–100 events are needed for decisiveness. Its firmest consequence is kinematic: δ_max rises to 387 keV.
