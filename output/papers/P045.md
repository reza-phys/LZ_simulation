# P045: What a lower limit that lifts off zero means for one event: coverage, flip-flopping and the Baxter et al. conventions
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-10
- Primary arXiv category: physics.data-an (cross-list hep-ex)
- Author profile: frequentist statisticians in particle physics
- Category (from your taxonomy): STAT
- Builds on / responds to: LZ arXiv:2609.02823 (Results, Fig. 6, LEE supplement, statistics paragraph); P001, P007, P008, P011, P012, P016, P017, P021

**Abstract.** LZ's two-sided 90% intervals lift off zero for many models because of one event. For one count on b ≈ 6×10⁻⁴ every proper construction gives a lower edge of 0.105 events (Feldman–Cousins, toy-calibrated t̃_μ, Δχ²) or 0.16–0.18 (Cowan's boundary-aware asymptotics); LZ's digitised edges, 0.31–0.38 (Fig. 6 bottom) and ≈0.2 (top), match none at 90%. A non-zero lower limit somewhere in the raster scan occurs in 25–35% of background-only experiments; it is a p_local < 0.10 statement.

**Question and approach.** (a) Which lower edge does a two-sided 90% construction give for n = 1 on b ∈ {0, 5.7×10⁻⁴, 10⁻³, 10⁻²}, and does spectral information change it? (b) What is the coverage of the interval reported without a discovery threshold, versus flip-flopping policies? (c) How often does a scan over ~12 effective windows (P008) produce a lifted-off lower limit under H₀? (d) How should "the lower limit is non-zero" be read? Tools: exact Neyman construction with FC ordering, toy-calibrated t̃_μ (3×10⁴ toys per μ), naive Δχ² = 2.706, Cowan et al.'s asymptotic t̃_μ cdf, the one-event likelihood e^{−s}(s+β), exact coverage enumeration, and a 12-bin toy with P008's NR-band background (2.87 events).

**Results.** *Edges (b = 5.7×10⁻⁴).* FC [0.105, 4.36]; toy-calibrated [0.106, 4.35]; Δχ² [0.105, 3.65]; Cowan-asymptotic [0.16–0.18, 3.65]; FC 68% [0.368, 2.75]; Δχ² = 1 [0.301, 2.36] (= Table I). The FC lower edge is −ln 0.9 − b, where the n = 0 toys alone fill 90%. Spectral information (β = b f_b/f_s at the event) only *lowers* the edge, to 0.1046. LZ's digitised bottom-panel edges, 0.31–0.38 events, equal the 68% lower edges; the top-panel ratios 0.050–0.061 give 0.18–0.27, matching the Cowan asymptotics. A continuous toy statistic splits the n = 1 atom: μ_lo = 0.105, 0.20, 0.28, 0.39 when a fraction f = 1, 0.5, 0.31, 0.15 of one-event toys is at least as extreme; 0.38 needs f = 0.17.

*Coverage.* FC never drops below 0.900; Δχ² under-covers for 56% of μ ∈ [0, 12] (minimum 0.694 at μ = 1.36, where its n = 0 limit 1.35 excludes true signals FC's 2.44 covers). With a 3σ threshold (b = 1, 3), FC-always (LZ) and Baxter-type upper-edge-only reporting stay ≥ 0.900; flip-flopping to one-sided limits dips to 0.855.

*Scan.* Per-window lift-off under H₀ is 0.12–8.7%; family-wise 0.0012 (M = 1), 0.077 (6), 0.254 (12); continuous statistics give 1 − 0.9^{N_eff} = 0.27–0.34. Under s = 1 the true window lifts off in 63% of experiments (coverage 0.919).

**Caveats.** Single-bin and 12-bin toys replace LZ's 2D likelihood with ~1700 events and nuisances; the digitised edges carry ±11% (d₁₀) and depend on P012's N(d₁₀ = 1) = 12.9; Baxter's 3σ-threshold and PCL recommendations are recalled (likely). Edge comparisons are provisional pending DR-001.

**Conclusion.** "The lower limit is non-zero" means p_local < 0.10 for that model; it arises in 25–35% of null scans and adds nothing to the 2.6σ global p. The 0.31–0.38-event bottom-panel edge is not a 90% edge of any standard construction on ŝ = 1; the Data Release should say which.
