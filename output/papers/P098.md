# P098: Energy resolution at 250 keV from quanta statistics: does a ±23 keV (9 %) uncertainty follow from N_q fluctuations, recombination and the NR-band width?

- Primary arXiv category: physics.ins-det
- Author profile: LXe detector-response group
- Category (from your taxonomy): RESP

**Abstract.** LZ quotes 248 ± 23 (stat) ± 23 (sys) keV without a resolution function. We build the statistical part from first principles: nestpy quanta fluctuations at fixed 248 keV plus a layered detector model, for S1-only, combined-quanta, S2-only and 2D estimators. The resolution is 11.4 keV (4.6 %), 10.2–12.4 keV over assumptions, dominated by photon-detection statistics; ±23 keV would need 4× the variance. The NR-band width converts to only 0.9 keV of energy.

**Question and approach.** For an NR at S1c = 540.1, S2c = 9268 phd, which terms set σ(E_reco), how does the log₁₀S2c band width relate to it through the S1–S2 anticorrelation, what are the S1-only and S2-only energies, what is the ER resolution, and how does σ_E propagate to δ_max? We sample nestpy 2.1.1 `GetQuanta` (LZ_WS2024 widths, Table S5 yields with the p(E) break, P009's contour scale N_q = 11.32 E^1.112) 120 000 times, add photon binomial (g₁ = 0.110), double-phe (0.214), SPE resolution (0.338), extraction (0.80), SE Fano (4), position residual (2 %) and e-lifetime (1.5 % on S2c) cumulatively, invert N_q(E), N_ph(E), N_e(E), and compute the 2D Fisher resolution from the MC covariance. Analytic marginals check every term.

**Results.** *Quanta.* At 248 keV N_ph = 4897, N_e = 301, N_q = 5198; sd(N_ph) = 44.7, sd(N_e) = 17.6, corr = −0.24, so sd(N_q) = 43.9 quanta = 1.9 keV. *Budget* (N_q estimator, marginal σ_E, keV): photon binomial 8.5, double-phe 5.4, position 4.2, SPE 2.8, quanta 1.9, S2-side terms < 0.4; total **11.4 keV (4.6 %)**; S1-only 11.6, 2D 11.2, S2-only 56; Table S5 scale 11.9; σ_pos 0/3/4 % → 10.7/12.4/13.6; no double-phe → 10.2 (P009/P024). σ_E = 11.5 (E/248)^0.54 keV, within 5 % of 11√(E/248). *±23 keV* needs σ(N_q) = 536 quanta (S1c spread 10.9 % vs 5.4 %), variance ×4.0, which no detector or NEST width parameter supplies; 2 × 11.45 = 22.9 keV is a 2σ half-width. *Band.* At fixed E, corr(S1c, log₁₀S2c) = −0.04 and sd(log₁₀S2c) = 0.0312 dex (P024 0.0313) = 21.7 electrons = **0.93 keV** in N_q; the 1.5σ deficit (31.6 e) is 1.35 keV (N_q) or 4.1 keV (2D: E_2D = 244.4; P009 245.8). *Event.* E_S1 = 248.6 ± 11.6, E_Nq = 247.2 ± 11.5, E_S2 = 176.7 ± 54 keV: 1.3σ apart. *ER* (248 keV β): σ_E = 6.4 keV = 2.6 % (recalled XENON1T 2.2 %). *Propagation.* σ(δ_max) = 0.4/2.5/3.5 keV (400/1000/4000 GeV) for 11.4 keV, 0.8/5.0/7.1 for 23; P021's peak ±3.5 vs ±7.1 keV.

**Caveats.** ε_ext, σ_pos, P_dphe and the e-lifetime term are assumed or from the nestpy object; the contour scale is P009's inference. NEST v2.4.5's NR width model is unknown to us beyond LZ_WS2024. The 2D resolution is Gaussian-Fisher. The recalled ER resolutions (2.2–4 %) bracket ours. The 2σ reading of "±23" is a hypothesis.

**Conclusion.** The statistical energy resolution of the LZ event is 11.4 keV (4.6 %), light-collection dominated and consistent with the band width, roll-off and 1.5σ statement; σ_E = 11.5 (E/248)^0.54 keV should replace 23 keV smearing, with LZ's ±23 (sys) treated as a scale nuisance.
