# P041: Detector artefacts: could partial charge loss turn a 71 keV electron recoil into the LZ event?
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-10
- Primary arXiv category: physics.ins-det
- Author profile: dual-phase TPC instrumentation experts
- Category (from your taxonomy): BKG
- Builds on / responds to: LZ paper (arXiv:2609.02823) detector, event-description, Discussion and waveform sections; P010 (ER quanta), P022 (drift geometry, accidentals), P024 (band width), P004, P009; dossier hypothesis F

**Abstract.** If the LZ candidate were a 69.6 keV electron recoil, 78 % of its 1226 ionisation electrons must have vanished after recombination with S1 and pulse shape intact. We size each instrumental route (lifetime, localised absorbers, field/extraction defects, clipping, truncation, mis-pairing). Each needs an effect 3–25× larger than any published detector scale, and an absorber likely to hit this event would populate the empty band below the ER cloud with ~100 events. The artefact class shrinks from 0.18 to ≲0.006.

**Question and approach.** Which mechanisms can remove a fraction 1 − f = 0.78 of drifting electrons (f = 269/1226), what spatial or temporal signature does each leave, which does LZ's own evidence exclude (83mKr-based S2c map, stable g2, S2 width matching drift, alpha-template shape, "no abnormalities" nearby), and what remains? We digitise Fig. 4 to locate the grey ER population's lower edge (log₁₀S2c = 4.37 at S1c = 540) and verify the band 4.15–4.30 is empty at S1c 520–800; define landing regions in f; model a Gaussian electron cloud (σ_T = 3.1 mm at 859 μs) meeting an absorbing disk; and count monitor events (β + α, 155 h⁻¹ in the FV) that any bulk transient would also affect.

**Results.** *Lifetime.* The S2c correction fails by ×0.22 at 859 μs only if τ_e = 566 μs; a mis-measured 2.5–10 ms lifetime loses 8–29 %. Under τ_e = 566 μs a 70 keV ER reaches the NR ± 2σ window only at drift 715–878 μs. The ROI sample cannot test a one-hour excursion; the 84 h⁻¹ β/α at drift > 500 μs can: P(no witness) = 0.25, 9×10⁻⁴, 10⁻³⁷ for 60 s, 300 s, 1 h, and τ must lie in 554–680 μs (p = 0.045).

*Local absorber.* A 5.4 mm absorbing disk gives 78 %; for 5–60 mm disks only 1–2 % of hits reach the NR window, the rest filling the empty regions. A 10 % chance of one event-like ER among 5261 ERs at S1c 250–600 needs an area fraction (0.8–1.6)×10⁻³ — one persistent 1–3 cm object, a 19–61σ hole in the 83mKr S2c map — and predicts 65–101 charge-deficient ERs. Transients survive with best-case P(1 in window, 0 elsewhere) = 0.04–0.06.

*Extraction, pulses.* The ×4.6 gain deficit is 18× the map amplitude; the alpha template (75–149× larger pulses) excludes clipping; truncation to 22 % of the area leaves σ = 0.48 σ_true versus a 10–20 % width tolerance; mis-pairing is P022's accidental (4.3σ). Residuals: bulk lifetime 10⁻⁶; transient absorber/field/extraction 1.6×10⁻³; clipping 2×10⁻⁵; mis-pairing and unknowns 4×10⁻³.

**Caveats.** Channel priors and survival factors are judgement, printed in full. The parent population (×0.5–2), diffusion, lifetime, Rn and 83mKr statistics are recalled. A perfectly absorbing disk is the geometry most favourable to a "just-right" loss; soft absorbers do worse. Fig. 4 pixel reading is ±0.02 dex.

**Conclusion.** No instrumental charge-loss mechanism reproduces the event without an unseen population or a violated published check; the residual artefact mass is ≈ 0.006, dominated by untestable unknowns. LZ can close it with the hour-resolved lifetime, the map cell at the event's (x,y), and the out-of-ROI strip.
