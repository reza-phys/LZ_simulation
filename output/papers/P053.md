# P053: ²¹⁴Pb in the mixed-flow state: what the unavailable radon tag costs and whether ²¹⁴Pb-driven MSSI could be elevated around 16 June 2023
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-11
- Primary arXiv category: physics.ins-det (cross-list hep-ex)
- Author profile: radon-background experts
- Category (from your taxonomy): BKG
- Builds on / responds to: LZ paper (arXiv:2609.02823) Discussion, Table I, supplement MSSI section; P004 (wall-MSSI factor), P029 (attenuation), P010, P041

**Abstract.** LZ's radon tag could not be applied to the 248 keV candidate because the detector was in the mixed-flow state. LZ's own HE-SB tag statistics put 33% (21–48%) of the exposure in mixed flow, so the coincidence is unremarkable. Table I implies a ²¹⁴Pb activity of 5 (3–7) μBq/kg. A ²¹⁴Pb-MSSI origin 27 cm from every charge-dead region needs a 10⁵–10⁷-fold transient elevation, while the ER rate bounds any day-long excursion to ×3.4. A negative tag would have carried a likelihood ratio of only 0.4.

**Question and approach.** (a) Mixed-flow fraction: 12 of 18 HE-SB RFR events fell in tag-active periods; Clopper–Pearson intervals, assuming flow-independent ²¹⁴Pb rates. (b) Why flow matters: radioactivedecay gives the chain timing; a daughter moves v·τ before decaying. (c) Activity and elevation: a Fermi β-spectrum folded through the NEST-LZ ROI window (1.35–18.9 keVee); 352/295/242 keV γ-rays (λ = 2.7/2.2/1.5 cm) ray-traced from the event to the 3 mm wall shell and the 13.75 cm RFR. (d) Tag likelihood ratios.

**Results.** Mixed-flow fraction 0.33 (68% 0.21–0.48; 95% 0.13–0.59), 73 (46–105) live days; tag efficiency 7/12 = 0.58 matches LZ's 60%; P(a random event is untaggable) = 0.33. The six mixed-flow HE-SB RFR events versus 21.5·f_m expected limit a mixed-flow RFR-MSSI enhancement to < 1.7 (95%) for f_m = 0.33, < 2.8 for f_m = 0.2.

Bulk throughput moves LXe only 0.6–2 cm per ²¹⁴Pb mean life (38.7 min), but ion drift at 96.5 V/cm (recalled mobility 10⁻⁴–10⁻³ cm² V⁻¹ s⁻¹) gives 0.2–2 m: flow fixes where ²¹⁴Pb decays, not how often. From 1341 × 0.7 ROI events, a 9.2% ground-state branch and 2.2% acceptance: 4.7 × 10⁵ decays per run, 5.2 (3.1–6.7) μBq/kg.

Geometry (Fig. 1): from the event a 352 keV γ reaches a dead region unscattered with probability 5.3 × 10⁻⁶ (295 keV 4.8 × 10⁻⁷; 242 keV 1.2 × 10⁻⁹) versus 6.9 × 10⁻³ at the FV edge. With a 10–14 keV β the per-decay topology probability is 1.0 × 10⁻⁸, 0.010 (0.002–0.045) of the FV average. The model reproduces LZ's HE-SB RFR count (14–18 vs 21.5, 5 cm bottom cut) and 0.0037 wall ROI MSSI versus 0.0048.

Elevation: P004's k = 619 becomes 880–2060 for the ²¹⁴Pb share alone; confined to 24 h (1 h) it is 2.7 × 10⁵ (6.5 × 10⁶), ×100 more with the position penalty. The ROI ER rate (7.8/day) detects at 3σ any k ≥ 3.4 over 24 h or k ≥ 22 over 1 h; the α rate (≈ 130/h) sees k = 2 at 5σ within 11 min (Fig. 2).

Tag: LR(negative) = 0.4/(1 − f_false) = 0.40–0.44; LR(positive) = 0.6/f_false = 6–60 for f_false = 10–1%.

**Caveats.** The mixed-flow fraction is event-weighted; a flow-dependent RFR-MSSI rate biases it (mapping in details). Branching ratios, attenuation lengths, ion mobility and the ²¹⁴Pb share of the internal-β events are recalled or assumed (activity ×0.6–1.3). Our Compton-escape treatment fails for RFR MSSI in the ROI (unused); the unknown 5.4 t bottom cut moves the HE-SB check ×4.

**Conclusion.** Mixed flow covered a third of the run, so the untagged candidate is unremarkable; the SS rate pins ²¹⁴Pb at 5 μBq/kg, and no transient short of ×10⁵ makes ²¹⁴Pb MSSI at the event's position a 10% explanation. LZ should publish the flow timeline and the ±24 h BiPo rate.
