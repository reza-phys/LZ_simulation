# P083: Base rates: single-event and few-event anomalies in direct detection (2008–2026) and other rare-event searches, and what they did next
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-16
- Primary arXiv category: physics.data-an (cross-list hep-ex)
- Author profile: history-and-sociology-of-physics group with a statistics group
- Category (from your taxonomy): BKG
- Builds on / responds to: LZ arXiv:2609.02823; dossier §4; P001, P027, P061 (P050, P069 for timelines)

**Abstract.** We compile from memory, with per-item reliability flags, 45 anomalies at or near 2.5–4σ in direct detection and other rare-event searches (1994–2025) and record their fates. Of 25 resolved new-physics anomalies none was real; of 5 resolved hints of a predicted process, 4 were. The direct-detection base rate is r = 0.06 (95 % < 0.23), coinciding with P061's prior median, and gives P(DM | LZ event) ≈ 0.01 (68 % 0.001–0.05), resolving in ~3 years.

**Question and approach.** How often did ~3σ local single-, few-event or small-excess anomalies become signal, how fast, and by what route? We list every case we recall with confidence (43 primary; 2 flagged uncertain and excluded), each with year, events, reported significance (unknown for 9), outcome (real / statistical / background / artefact / contradicted / theory revision / unresolved), time to resolution and route. Base rates are Beta posteriors (Jeffreys prior) over resolved items; unresolved cases are counted as null or real for brackets. Time to resolution is a Kaplan–Meier curve censored at September 2026. The LZ posterior uses P061 Eq. 3, P = rB′/(rB′ + 1 − r), B′ = (B_DM/f_FP)/14.6, with P027's B_DM.

**Results.** *Compilation* (Fig. 1). 11 direct-detection items (DAMA/LIBRA, CoGeNT, CDMS-II Si/Ge, CRESST-II, XENON100, XENON1T ER, cryogenic LEE, ¹²⁴Xe DEC, ⁸B CEνNS) and 34 others (750 GeV, LSND/MiniBooNE, EDGES, R_K, KM3NeT, …). Outcomes (43): 9 statistical, 7 contradicted, 4 artefact, 3 background, 3 theory revision, 4 real, 13 unresolved. Survival tracks one covariate, a *predicted* process: new physics 0 real of 25 resolved (in-window 0/13); predicted processes 4/5.

*Base rates.* DD new physics 0/7 → r = 0.062 (68 % 0.003–0.13; 95 % < 0.23); all new physics 0/25 → 0.019 (< 0.073). Recall bias (f_null/f_real = 0.33–1) lowers DD to 0.022–0.062; dilution by unpublished excursions does not apply to a published event.

*Survival* (Fig. 2). With a comparable independent test (n = 24): 25/46/54/71 % resolved by 1/2/3/5 yr, median 3 yr; without (n = 11), 27 % by 5 yr. Routes: independent experiment 52 %, more data 16 %, reanalysis 12 %, theory 12 %.

*LZ* (new physics, single event, xenon tests available): P(DM) = 0.012 (0.001–0.052) at B_DM = 16.4, f_FP = 3; 0.059 (0.005–0.22) at 28.7, f_FP = 1; applying B_DM directly (0.34–0.48) double-counts. Implied π_DM = 0.009 (< 0.08); P061's median 0.017 ↔ r = 0.055; P(r ≥ 0.25, P061's P > 0.5 threshold) = 4 %. Resolution: 46 % by 2028, median 2029, likeliest via XENONnT/PandaX.

**Caveats.** The compilation is from memory and incomplete; 9 significances are unrecalled and 2 items are excluded as uncertain. Outcome coding is judgement ("contradicted" counts as not real; recoding it as unresolved gives 0/18, r = 0.026). Zero-count posteriors are prior-driven (Jeffreys → uniform ×1.8). The DD-only stratum has n = 8. Times are ±0.5 yr. Selection corrections are parametrised, not measured.

**Conclusion.** The outside view gives the LZ event a prior of ~6 % (< 23 %) of being real, equal to P061's central prior, so the corpus's P(DM) ≈ 0.01–0.02 is the historical answer. The decisive covariate, a predicted rate, is absent; the decisive remedy, an independent xenon test, is available.
