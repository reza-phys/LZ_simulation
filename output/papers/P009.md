# P009: 248 or 265 keV? Reconstructing the recoil energy of the LZ event with the tuned NEST model
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-04
- Primary arXiv category: physics.ins-det (cross-list hep-ex)
- Author profile: liquid-xenon detector-response modellers
- Category (from your taxonomy): RESP
- Builds on / responds to: LZ paper arXiv:2609.02823; corpus dossier 00 (§2 checks i, ii)

**Abstract.** We reconstruct the recoil energy of LZ's (540.1 phd, 9268 phd) event with the tuned NEST model (Table S5) in nestpy 2.1.1 via S1, S2, combined-quanta and 2D-likelihood estimators. Published parameters give 262 ± 11 keV, but LZ's own energy contours and 269.9 keV efficiency edge imply 8% more S1 per keV (Nq ≈ 11.3 E^1.11), giving 246 ± 10 keV, ε = 0.93. The paper's 248 keV is self-consistent; "265 keV" is a reproduction artefact.

**Question and approach.** Does S1c = 540.1 phd mean 248 or 265 keV, and how close is the event to the 269.9 keV efficiency edge and the S1c < 600 phd cut? We evaluate Table S5 (with/without the p(E) break) and NEST defaults in nestpy, invert S1, S2 and Nq = S1c/g1 + S2c/g2, and build a 2D likelihood in (S1c, log10 S2c) from NEST `GetQuanta` fluctuations plus binomial S1 detection, binomial extraction, SE Fano and a 2% position-correction residual (20 000 samples per 2 keV step, 180–320 keV). Independently we digitise Figs. 2 and 4 (event recovered at 539.8 phd, 3.970) for the collaboration's own S1c–energy relation, and fit the Fig. S2 roll-off.

**Results.** *Public parameters.* Table S5 gives S1c of 498 phd at 248 keV and 549 phd at 270 keV; E_S1 = 266.2, E_comb = 264.4 keV; S2 alone is uninformative. The 2D likelihood gives **E_ML = 262.3 keV (68% 251–273)**, shifting 3.9/1.0 keV for g1/g2; ε(E_ML) = 0.71 and MC E50 = 291 keV.

*The paper's own scale.* The Fig. 4 contours cross the NR median at 298.8, 418.8, 543.8, 671.1 phd (150–300 keV_nr); their keVee labels imply constant S1c/g1 + S2c/g2 (W = 13.46 eV) and Nq = 11.32 E^1.112, 7.7% above Table S5 at 250 keV (β = 1.11 recovers 5.7%). The event interpolates to 248.5 keV. With this scale **E_ML = 245.8 keV (68% 236–255)**, the MC roll-off matches Fig. S2 (E50 = 271.7 keV), ε(E_ML) = 0.93 and P(S1c > 600) = 0.7%.

*Band position.* For a flat spectrum at S1c = 540 ± 5 the MC median is 4.015 (σ 0.033 dex): the event is 1.46σ low, matching the paper's 1.5σ, whereas the drawn 10–90% lines (σ 0.022 dex) would make it 2.5σ. Our 10 keV statistical width, half the paper's, matches the roll-off σ_E = 11.8 keV.

*Consequences.* δ_max(1000 GeV, June) is 386.6 vs 389.9 keV for 248 vs 265 keV; E_ee = 69.7 keVee, above the 67.3/64.3 keV lines.

**Caveats.** The 8% offset is inferred from digitised figures (0.2% axis precision) and the efficiency edge, not from the Data Release; its cause (β rounding, W convention, a NEST v2.4.5 feature absent from nestpy 2.1.1) is not established. The detector-fluctuation model is ours (σ_pos 0–4% shifts E_ML < 1 keV, σ by ± 2 keV). NEST fluctuations at 250 keV are extrapolated.

**Conclusion.** On LZ's own energy scale the event is a 246 ± 10 keV recoil on the efficiency plateau, not at its edge; users of public Table S5 + nestpy should rescale Nq by ≈ +8% (or take β ≈ 1.11) before computing efficiencies or keVee equivalents near the ROI.
