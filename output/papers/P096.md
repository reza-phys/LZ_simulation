# P096: Large liquid-scintillator detectors as high-energy recoil detectors: can Borexino, KamLAND, JUNO or SNO+ see the LZ models through ¹²C, ¹³C and ¹H recoils?
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-16
- Primary arXiv category: hep-ex (cross-list hep-ph)
- Author profile: large-liquid-scintillator (neutrino-detector) group
- Category (from your taxonomy): COMP
- Builds on / responds to: LZ arXiv:2609.02823; P046, P067 (targets, ceilings), P012, P003 (L10), P021 (inelastic fit), P057, P058 (exothermic χ₂)

**Abstract.** Kiloton scintillators have 10³–10⁴ times LZ's mass but only H, C and O nuclei. At v_max = 809 km/s the inelastic ceilings are 3.4/40/44/54 keV on ¹H/¹²C/¹³C/¹⁶O, so δ = 300–380 keV (A ≥ 97–126) is forbidden. LZ's L10 fit gives 0.8 ¹³C recoils per year in JUNO at 3–9 keV_ee. Only χ₂ down-scattering on carbon is open: a ~300 keV quasi-line, 10⁵ events/yr in JUNO at LZ's f₂ limit, under 10¹² ¹⁴C decays.

**Question and approach.** For (A) inelastic isoscalar O₁ at δ = 300/350/366/380 keV, (B) elastic L10 and O6 at LZ's best fit, (C) SI at σ_n = 10⁻⁴⁷ cm² and (D) P058's exothermic χ₂, what rates, quenched energies and ¹⁴C signal-to-background follow in Borexino (278 t PC), KamLAND (1 kt), SNO+ (780 t LAB) and JUNO (20 kt LAB)? WimPyDD rates on ¹H (validated analytically to 3–6 %), ¹²C, ¹⁶O, Xe; ¹³C from a single-particle p₁/₂ estimate (WimPyDD has no ¹³C responses); models normalised to one accepted LZ event; exothermic coupling fixed by the endothermic count (P058's R_ROI reproduced to 1 %); Birks quenching and a Fermi-function ¹⁴C spectrum (recalled inputs).

**Results.** *(A)* An endothermic scatter needs v ≥ √(2δ/μ) = 2209 km/s on ¹²C and 7612 km/s on ¹H at δ = 300 keV, 2.7–9.4× v_max: forbidden at any mass. *(B)* On a free proton |M|²_avg = 2c²q⁴/m_N⁴ (L10) and c₆²q⁴/(16m_N⁴) (O6), E_R ≤ 13.5 keV: L10 at LZ's fit gives 1.1 × 10⁻⁷ events per tonne of hydrogen per year (median 4.6 keV → ~1 keV_ee). ¹³C (⟨S_n⟩ = −1/6, E_R ≤ 172 keV) is 350× larger per tonne of LAB: 0.80/yr in JUNO, 0.011 in Borexino, at 3–9 keV_ee. *(C)* 58 carbon recoils/yr in JUNO at 1–6 keV_ee. *(D)* Because μ ≈ m_N, down-scattering on light nuclei is a quasi-line at E* = |δ|μ/m_N: 297 keV on ¹²C (16–84 %: 249–371 keV), 300 keV on ¹H (283–319), against xenon's broad 73–206 keV (P058). At P058's f₂ limits JUNO sees 1.6–2.0 × 10⁵ carbon and 64–88 proton recoils per year, Borexino 2.3–2.9 × 10³. Quenched (Q_C ≈ 0.05, Q_p ≈ 0.15) to 12–20 and 44–64 keV_ee, they face 7 × 10¹⁰ (JUNO, 10⁻¹⁷) and 2.7 × 10⁸ (Borexino) ¹⁴C decays per year in the carbon window: S/B = 2–10 × 10⁻⁶. JUNO's statistics-only one-year reach, f₂ = 6.0 × 10⁻³ to 2.9 × 10⁻⁷, is only 1.7–2.1× LZ's limits; a 10⁻³ systematic on ¹⁴C makes it 360–560× weaker. Every signal fails every recalled threshold except the ~1/yr proton line in Borexino (Fig. 1; full table in details.md).

**Caveats.** Masses, thresholds, ¹⁴C levels, Birks constants and stopping powers are recalled (quenching ×0.6–1.7); ¹³C uses a single-particle spin (factor ≈ 2); no ¹⁴C pile-up or pulse-shape model; κ inherits the endothermic halo dependence (κ(380) 45 % above P021); one operator per class, 1 TeV.

**Conclusion.** Scintillators cannot test any LZ interpretation: the inelastic models cannot scatter, the q⁴-spin models yield ≲ 1 sub-10 keV_ee recoil per year, and the one open channel — χ₂ down-scattering, a δ-measuring line at 12–20 keV_ee — is below threshold and 10⁶ times under ¹⁴C; only a ¹⁴C-free light target could use it.
