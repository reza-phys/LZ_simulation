# P077: S1 pulse-shape discrimination at 550 phd: what singlet/triplet ratios, photon statistics and propagation in a 1.5 m TPC allow, and why LZ called it inconclusive
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-15
- Primary arXiv category: physics.ins-det
- Author profile: LXe scintillation-timing experts
- Category (from your taxonomy): RESP
- Builds on / responds to: LZ arXiv:2609.02823 (supplement "Waveform Analysis"); P009, P010, P022, P024, P041, P042

**Abstract.** LZ calls S1 pulse-shape discrimination "inconclusive" for its 540 phd candidate. In a toy S1 model (singlet/triplet emission, optical Monte Carlo of a 1.5 m PTFE-lined TPC tuned to g₁ = 0.11, 10 ns sampling) textbook singlet/triplet brackets separate ER from NR by 1.4–8σ at 540 phd. LZ's statement that 150 keV AmBe recoils match an ER template therefore bounds the intrinsic ER–NR timing difference to ≲ 2 ns and any single-event PSD likelihood ratio to ≲ 2–5.

**Question and approach.** How much ER/NR information does a 540-photon S1 carry, why did LZ get none, and how strong is the depth (RFR-versus-FV) argument? We track 2×10⁵ photons per depth through a cylinder (r = 72.8 cm, 145.6 cm drift, Rayleigh 35 cm, PTFE R = 0.95, total internal reflection, grids, arrays) for propagation-time and top/bottom distributions; then simulate 10⁴ pulses per hypothesis with emission times f_s Exp(τ_s) + (1−f_s) Exp(τ_t) (optional recombination delay), 1.5 ns TTS and Poisson N = 540 or 297 (150 keV NR). Statistics: prompt fraction f_p(20 ns), mean time, and an ideal position-matched template likelihood ratio profiled over start time. All timing constants are recalled and bracketed.

**Results.** *Optics.* Photons travel ≈ 1 m per PTFE bounce: at z = 26.4 cm the propagation time has mean 27 ns, sd 29 ns; 21 % of the light is top (TBA −0.58; −0.84 in the RFR). This spread halves the point-detector separation (8.7σ → 4.5σ).

*Brackets at 540 phd.* NR I_s/I_t = 1.5 (τ_t 24 ns) versus drift-field ER 0.5 (26 ns): Δ⟨t_emit⟩ = 6.9 ns, d = 3.4σ (f_p), 3.7σ (mean time, digitised or not), 4.5σ (ideal LR). Closest pair (NR 1.0/27 ns, ER 0.6/24 ns): 1.4σ, LR 3.1. Zero-field-like 45 ns recombination: ≥ 7σ. d ∝ √N (d(540)/d(297) = 1.34).

*LZ's statement.* Scanning the NR singlet fraction, d(540) ≈ 0.6σ per ns of mean-time difference. If n = 5/10/30 AmBe recoils are "not inconsistent" with an ER template (shift < 2σ), then Δ⟨t⟩ < 2.0/1.5/1.0 ns, d(540) < 1.2/0.85/0.44, LR at the NR median < 2.1/1.4/1.1; allowing for LZ's energy and position mismatches, LR ≲ 2–5.

*Position.* From z = 26 to 100 cm the mean time shifts by 8.4 ns, 1.3× the ER–NR difference; templates must match to ±5 cm (residual 0.7σ).

*RFR origin.* The direct-path gain is 1.9 ns, but the early-light fraction changes: rise time 16.1 vs 25.6 ns (6.2σ), f_p 0.49 vs 0.31 (7.1σ), TBA −0.84 vs −0.58 (7.6σ).

**Caveats.** Singlet/triplet ratios, lifetimes, reflectances and the Rayleigh length are recalled (9 items uncertain); the optics is a tuned toy, not LZ's; the AmBe bound assumes a 2σ-shift criterion and ignores that the ¹³¹ᵐXe template is at 164 keV. Separations move ≤ 10 % between baseline and low-reflectivity optics.

**Conclusion.** PSD at 540 phd fails because 70 keV ERs and 250 keV NRs at 97 V/cm differ by ≲ 2 ns in mean emission time; a single-event verdict shifts ER-versus-NR odds by ≲ 2–5 and is silent on wall MSSI; depth discrimination is > 6σ.
