# P072: Exothermic scattering of a metastable excited state as the origin of the LZ 248 keV event: kinematics, spectral shape, companions and the required relic fraction
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-15
- Primary arXiv category: hep-ph
- Author profile: dark-matter phenomenology group
- Category (from your taxonomy): IDM
- Builds on / responds to: LZ arXiv:2609.02823 (Fig. 4, S2/S4, MSSI table); competes with P058 §5.6; uses P011, P016, P021, P026, P034, P038, P066

**Abstract.** If the excited state χ₂ of inelastic dark matter survives with relic fraction f₂ ≈ 0.5 (P026), its down-scattering χ₂N → χ₁N has no velocity threshold and could produce a 248 keV recoil. Using exact WimPyDD spectra for m = 0.3–10 TeV, |δ| = 100–500 keV and an observed-energy likelihood over LZ's regions, we find 248 keV above the 98th percentile everywhere, 2–7 companions in the empty 125–200 keV bin per event, and Bayes factors 0.04–0.67 against the endothermic reading.

**Question and approach.** Could a metastable χ₂ give one event at 248 keV in 2.84 t·yr, nothing in 5.4–200 keV and nothing in 600–1700 phd? WimPyDD's O₁ per-stream kernel is, isotope by isotope, a step function in v, so nine single-isotope tables give exact kernels for any (m, δ, halo) (10⁻¹⁶ agreement with P058's cached kernels; R_ROI = 915.5/859.1 vs P058 916/859, P011 858). Accepted spectra are smeared with σ_E = 11√(E/248) keV and fitted with a five-term likelihood: Poisson counts in 5.4–125 (42/58.6), 125–200 (0/0.02) and 600–1700 phd (0/0.61) plus the extended likelihood of the event at its energy over b_H = 5.7×10⁻⁴; Bayes factors use a flat prior on the 200–270 keV expectation ∈ [0, 10] (P016).

**Results.** *Kinematics.* E* = |δ|μ/m_N = 248 keV needs |δ| = 349/278/256/251 keV at 0.3/1/4/10 TeV, but the half-width μv√(2μ|δ|)/m_N grows with mass (138 → 230 keV): heavier χ broadens the line. *Shape.* Over the whole grid the accepted spectrum's mode is ≤ 186 keV and its 84th/95th percentiles ≤ 217/237 keV; 248 keV sits at the 97.9–99.9th percentile (99.7th at E* = 248). Per 200–270 keV event: N(125–200) = 5.7/5.4/4.5/2.1 at |δ| = 278/300/350/500 keV (1 TeV), N(5.4–125) = 5.1/2.9/0.75/0.04, N(600–1700 phd) = 0.8–2.7. *Likelihood.* P058's four-bin Z = 2.9–3.1 is reproduced; with the event's energy Z = 2.45–2.73 and the fitted signal is 0.11–0.17 events. B(exo/background) ≤ 1.96, 0.60 at E* = 248; endothermic B = 4.2 (350 keV), 10 with the June date. B(exo/endo) = 0.47 best-vs-best (0.21 with date), 0.04–0.67 over priors; a two-population mixture peaks at f₂ → 0, δ = 350 keV. *Halos.* v₀ = 50–150 km/s changes nothing; only a co-moving disk (lag ≤ 50, σ ≤ 20 km/s) gives a 32–43 keV line with companions ≤ 0.15, but the unavoidable halo χ₂ at ρ_d/ρ₀ ≤ 0.25 caps B at 2.0. *Coupling.* One event needs σ_N f₂ = 2.4×10⁻⁴⁵ cm² (1 TeV, 300 keV), 390× below the endothermic fit; χ₁ up-scatters 10⁻³ times; τ ≥ 1.4 t_U; dark photon m_A′ ≤ 5.6 GeV.

**Caveats.** One-dimensional observed-energy proxy of LZ's 2D likelihood; digitised low-energy counts (P016; ±60 % on B_exo at 300 keV) and P038's extrapolated 1700 phd edge; O₁ isoscalar (proton-only checked); Bayes factors depend on the signal prior (variants quoted); dark-disk density bounds are recalled.

**Conclusion.** We agree with P058's numbers to ≤ 1 % but sharpen the verdict: no mass, splitting or halo dispersion makes 248 keV a typical down-scatter; the hypothesis earns B ≤ 2 against background and 0.04–0.67 against the endothermic reading; only a co-moving disk helps, capped at B ≈ 2.
