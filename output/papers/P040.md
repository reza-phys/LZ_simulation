# P040: Cosmic-ray-boosted and otherwise fast light dark matter at the extended LZ window: can a relativistic light particle leave a lone 248 keV recoil?
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-09
- Primary arXiv category: hep-ph (cross-list astro-ph.HE)
- Author profile: boosted-dark-matter phenomenologists
- Category (from your taxonomy): EXO
- Builds on / responds to: LZ paper (arXiv:2609.02823) Data Analysis, Table I, Fig. 5; dossier hypothesis K; P003 (lone-event criterion), P017 (form factors), P019 (relativistic-beam argument), P013, P016, P030

**Abstract.** Sub-GeV dark matter boosted by cosmic rays (CRDM) or decays reaches xenon with β ~ 0.1–1 and a recoil spectrum flat in E_R, so it might leave a 248 keV recoil without a low-energy population. It cannot: for any velocity-independent contact interaction, the number of 5.4–55 keV events per 200–270 keV event is bounded below by the nuclear response alone, N_lo ≥ 350 (Helm) or 1184 (shell model). CRDM needs σ_χp ≈ 4–6×10⁻³⁰ cm², inside recalled exclusions.

**Question and approach.** For m_χ = 1 MeV–10 GeV we derive exact two-body kinematics, E_R,max = 2m_N(T² + 2m_χT)/[(m_χ+m_N)² + 2m_NT], and fold beam, power-law and self-consistent CRDM spectra with P003's efficiency and with the Helm (lzcommon) and shell-model (P017/WimPyDD) M responses; amplitude structures for vector, scalar and pseudoscalar mediators are included. The CRDM flux follows Bringmann–Pospelov (PDG proton spectrum, D_eff = 1–10 kpc, ρ = 0.3 GeV cm⁻³); the LZ rate in 200–270 keV for 4.71 t × 220 d gives σ_χp for one event. We compute Earth attenuation in 1478 m of rock and the spectrum of a fast TeV sub-population.

**Results.** *Kinematics.* T_min(248 keV) = 122, 114, 59, 7.7 and 0.89 MeV for m_χ = 1, 10, 100 MeV, 1 and 10 GeV (p ≥ 123 MeV/c); the neutron check gives 8.17 MeV (P013: 8.20).

*Floor on N_lo.* Each incident energy yields a flat spectrum truncated at its own E_R,max, and truncation can only deplete the high window, so for any incident spectrum N_lo ≥ ∫F²ε(5.4–55)/∫F²ε(200–270). With F²(30 keV)/F²(248 keV) = 1249 (Helm) and 6809 (shell) the floor is 350 (Helm) / 1184 (shell). Power laws and the vector-mediator endpoint suppression only raise it. Explicit q-dependence helps as in P003: q² (scalar mediator, m_χ ≤ 10 MeV) gives 33/114, q⁴ 4.1/14, q⁴-spin Σ″ 0.18. The SHM adds ×2.3 (820/2752): boosting removes the velocity penalty, not the form-factor one.

*CRDM.* One window event needs σ_χp = 3.6, 4.4, 6.3×10⁻³⁰ cm² (1, 10, 100 MeV; ×1.85 shell, ×0.32 at 10 kpc) and 2.4×10⁻²⁹ at 1–10 GeV: 40–60× inside the recalled XENON1T-recast band (10⁻³¹–10⁻²⁸) and predicting 2×10³–6×10⁴ events in LZ's 5.4–55 keV ROI, exceeding the whole 1710-event science sample. Rock is transparent below (1–4)×10⁻²⁸ cm² for m_χ ≤ 100 MeV; at ≥ 1 GeV the required σ exceeds the 30 %-loss ceiling (4×10⁻³⁰), so they never arrive.

*Heavy fast component.* TeV DM at β = 0.005–0.02: E_R,max = 4.9–92 MeV, N_lo = 350/1184.

**Caveats.** Cross-sections use the constant-amplitude convention and coherent A² scaling; the CRDM flux uses the PDG power law extended to 0.1 GeV without helium (×2–3 in flux, ×1.5 in σ). Exclusion bands are recalled (uncertain ×3); the LZ low-energy excess argument does not depend on them. Attenuation uses a mean-loss approximation. The 3–5 tolerance is P003's recalled value.

**Conclusion.** Speed does not buy a lone recoil: any contact-interaction beam gives ≥ 350–1184 low-energy events per window event, CRDM needs excluded cross-sections and floods LZ's low-energy ROI, and a fast TeV component fails identically. Only q⁴-spin couplings pass, as in the halo case.
