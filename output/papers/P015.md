# P015: Fluorine, argon, germanium, iodine and tungsten: which targets can confirm an inelastic 300–390 keV splitting?
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-05
- Primary arXiv category: hep-ph (cross-list hep-ex)
- Author profile: multi-target direct-detection phenomenologists
- Category (from your taxonomy): XEXP
- Builds on / responds to: LZ arXiv:2609.02823 (Fig. S7, Introduction citations, Table I); P002 (kinematics), P003 (coupling convention), P005 (xenon world), P007 (Higgsino δ = 366 keV); overlaps with P046 (later)

**Abstract.** An inelastic recoil needs δ < μv_max²/2. With v_max = 809 km/s (16 June) this ceiling at m_χ = 1 TeV is 63 keV for fluorine, 131 for argon, 232 for germanium, 385 for iodine, 396 for xenon and 533 keV for tungsten: every target lighter than A ≈ 96 is blind to δ = 300 keV at any WIMP mass. At equal coupling I/Xe = 0.59 → 0.02 and W/Xe = 16 → 700 for δ = 300 → 380 keV. No existing non-xenon exposure could contain LZ's signal; 10 kg·yr of CaWO₄ with an MeV recoil window could.

**Question and approach.** Which nuclei can scatter at all for the LZ-favoured splittings, at what rate relative to xenon, and what do PICO-60, DEAP-3600, CRESST and NaI exposures imply? We compute δ_max and the window [E₋, E₊] for ten nuclei at 400–4000 GeV; WimPyDD O₁ˢ window rates (unit coupling, annual-mean and June halos) at δ = 250–380 keV; normalise xenon to LZ's 1.0 event with the paper's efficiency; scale by recalled, flagged exposures; and digitise the Fig. S7 curves.

**Results.** *Kinematics.* At 1 TeV, δ_max = 40 (C), 54 (O), 63 (F), 76 (Na), 131 (Ar, Ca), 232 (Ge), 385 (I), 396 (Xe), 533 keV (W), nearly mass-independent (μ → m_N). A live target at δ = 250/300/350/380 keV needs A ≥ 79/96/114/125: **C₃F₈, argon and germanium contain zero events for every δ ≥ 250 keV**; PICO-60 C₃F₈ and DEAP-3600 are irrelevant. Windows at δ = 300 keV: Xe 91–786, I 97–745, W 52–1255 keV; LZ's 270 keV ROI captures 82/45/17/2.8% of the xenon window rate at δ = 300/350/366/380 keV.
*Rates (annual, per tonne of element, unit coupling)*: Xe 5831/209/44/8.7 per t·yr at δ = 300/350/366/380 keV; I/Xe = 0.59/0.30/0.14/0.023; W/Xe = 16/82/222/699 (A² explains only 2.0; the rest is halo phase space).
*Existing exposures at the LZ best fit* ((c₁ˢm_v²)² = 7.4 × 10⁻⁵, σ_SI = 2.2 × 10⁻⁴² cm² at 300 keV), δ = 300/350/366 keV: PICO-60 CF₃I (0.0024 t·yr iodine) 6–7 × 10⁻⁴; CRESST-II (9 × 10⁻⁵ t·yr tungsten) 6 × 10⁻⁴/0.006/0.04; DAMA/LIBRA (2.08 t·yr iodine) 0.53/0.49/0.60; COSINE-100 (0.17 t·yr) 0.04–0.05. Only DAMA/LIBRA is competitive in counts, but its 0.5 events fall in 9–67 keVee (Q_I ≈ 0.09) among ~5 × 10⁷ undiscriminated background counts. *Future*: 3 events need 14 t·yr of NaI, 7 t·yr of xenon (790 keV window), or CaWO₄ 0.68 t·yr (δ = 300), 0.073 t·yr (350), 10 kg·yr (366), 0.5 kg·yr (380 keV), with a recoil ROI to ≈ 1.2 MeV. *Fig. S7*: the xenon curves end at 231–303 keV; CRESST-II 2016 and PICO 2023 reach 350 keV but sit 1.2 × 10⁴/3.1 × 10³ (300 keV) and 1.3 × 10³/490 (350 keV) above LZ's upper edge.

**Caveats.** Exposures are recalled (CRESST, PICO CF₃I ±50%); counts assume unit efficiency over the whole window (ceilings). Tungsten uses WimPyDD's Helm M form factor, not a shell-model response (×2 on W/Xe; kinematics unaffected). O₁ only; the June halo raises W by 25%, I by 47%.

**Conclusion.** Confirming a 300–390 keV splitting requires A ≳ 100 nuclei. Iodine matches xenon at δ ≤ 300 keV but NaI lacks discrimination; tungsten is 16–700× better per tonne, so a CaWO₄ detector at 10 kg·yr–0.7 t·yr with an MeV ROI is the decisive non-xenon test.
