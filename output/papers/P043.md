# P043: Propagating the LZ gain uncertainties: what g₁ ± 2 % and g₂ ± 3 % do to the recoil energy, the band position, δ_max and the fitted couplings
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-10
- Primary arXiv category: physics.ins-det
- Author profile: calibration and systematics specialists
- Category (from your taxonomy): RESP
- Builds on / responds to: LZ arXiv:2609.02823 (detector paragraph, Results, Table S5); P009, P024, P038, P021, P012, P002, P007

**Abstract.** LZ quotes 248 ± 23 (stat) ± 23 (sys) keV without decomposing the systematic. We propagate g₁ = 0.110 ± 0.002, g₂ = 34.5 ± 1.1, a ± 4 % NR light-yield scale, the drift field and W through three energy estimators, the NR-band position, the efficiency, δ_max and the couplings, linearly (`uncertainties`) and by Monte Carlo. The gains give 3.8 keV, the yield scale 8.7 keV; closing ± 23 keV needs a 10 % light-yield uncertainty.

**Question and approach.** How is the ± 23 keV shared between g₁, g₂, the yield model (Table S5 vs LZ's contour scale, +7.7 % in N_q), W and the field, and how do the same nuisances move the "1.5σ below the NR median", ε at the event, δ_max(m), P021's likelihood peak and the couplings d₁₀ (P012) and κ̂ (P021)? We use P009's contour scale N_q = s·11.32 E^1.112 with N_e from Table S5 (nestpy), s ~ N(1, 0.04) (half the Table-S5 offset), F ± 5 V/cm, W = 13.5 ± 0.2 eV and band width 0.0313 ± 0.004 dex (P024); estimators S1-only, combined quanta and a 2D Gaussian-likelihood proxy; `uncertainties.wrap` for the linear part, 10⁵ draws for the full.

**Results.** *Energy.* dlnE/dln g₁ = −0.84, dlnE/dln s = −0.89 (β_eff = dlnS1c/dlnE = 1.16); g₂'s 2D weight is 3.7 %. Budget for E_ML (keV): g₁ 3.7, g₂ 0.8, N_q-scale 8.7, field 0.04; total 9.6. The gains alone cannot give 23 keV; σ_s = 10.4 % would be required, about the full Table-S5 shift.

*Band.* Anchored to P024's 1.54σ: σ = 0.54 (g₂ 0.44, width 0.22, N_q 0.17, g₁ 0.07); P(> 2σ) = 0.20, P(< 1σ) = 0.14.

*Efficiency.* E50 = 272.2 keV moves ∓ 4.3 (g₁) and ∓ 10 keV (s), yet ε at the reconstructed energy is 0.951 ± 0.010 (plateau only): event and edge are both set by S1c, so the scale cancels; 1/ε = 1.06 (1.04–1.11).

*Kinematics and couplings.* δ_max(1 TeV, June) = 386.6 ± 2.1 ± 2.1 keV; P021's peak, 0.31 keV per keV of E_obs, is 380 ± 3 ± 3 keV. Via the acceptance, d₁₀(1 TeV) = 0.279 ± 0.005; κ̂ changes < 1 % for δ ≤ 350 keV but ×/÷ 1.14 (g₁) and 1.30 (s) at δ = 380 keV.

| quantity | value ± stat ± sys |
|---|---|
| E_R (contour scale) | 245.3 ± 9.8 ± 9.6 keV |
| σ below NR median | 1.54 ± 0.54 |
| d₁₀ˢ(1 TeV) | 0.28 (0.15–0.43) ± 0.005 |

**Caveats.** The ± 4 % yield prior, field bracket and plateau ± 0.01 are assumed. The 2D estimator is a Gaussian proxy with P038's S1c width; band shifts are anchored to P024's MC; P021's slope rests on a 5 keV grid; W acts on keVee only (degenerate with s on the NR scale); N_e is held fixed under s.

**Conclusion.** The quoted gains explain 3.8 keV of LZ's 23 keV systematic; the remainder must be the NR light-yield model (≈ 10 %), which leaves acceptance, δ_max and d₁₀ untouched but puts ± 0.5σ on the band position and ± 15–30 % on κ̂ at δ ≥ 370 keV.
