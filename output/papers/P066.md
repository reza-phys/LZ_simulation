# P066: A 300–380 keV line from the sky: what INTEGRAL/SPI, COMPTEL and future MeV telescopes say about a long-lived excited dark-matter state
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-12
- Primary arXiv category: astro-ph.HE (cross-list hep-ph)
- Author profile: MeV γ-ray astronomers
- Category (from your taxonomy): COMP
- Builds on / responds to: LZ arXiv:2609.02823; P026 (f₂ after freeze-out, SPI/EGB), P011 (dark photon, exothermic bound), P014, P023, P042

**Abstract.** If a fraction f₂ of the dark matter still sits in the excited state χ₂, its radiative decay paints a narrow δ ≈ 300–380 keV line over the sky with flux ∝ f₂/τ_γ. We compute halo D-factors, fluxes per region and the redshifted continuum against the CXB. Recalled SPI sensitivities give f₂/τ_γ < (0.5–5)×10⁻²³ s⁻¹; combined with LZ's exothermic bound, any LZ-fitting χ₂ must have BR_γ ≲ 10⁻³. The Higgsino and the dark photon predict no line.

**Question and approach.** P026 leaves f₂ = 0.42–0.50 excited after freeze-out, and P011/P026 note that LZ itself limits f₂(today) ≤ 1.2×10⁻³ (δ = 300 keV) through exothermic down-scattering. What does the MeV sky add? The halo line flux is Φ_Ω = f₂D_Ω/(4πm_χτ_γ), D_Ω = ∫dΩ∫ρ dl. We integrate NFW (r_s = 20 kpc), Einasto (α = 0.17) and Burkert (r_c = 9 kpc) profiles, all normalised to ρ_⊙ = 0.3 GeV cm⁻³, on a HEALPix grid validated against scipy cone integrals; bracket recalled 3σ narrow-line sensitivities of SPI, COSI and e-ASTROGAM/AMEGO; compute the isotropic continuum dI/dE = cΓn₀/[4πE H(z)] against the Gruber et al. CXB; and place the corpus models in the (τ_γ, f₂) plane.

**Results.** D(10° cone) = 7.48×10²¹ GeV cm⁻² sr (NFW; P026: 7.41×10²¹), 9.3×10²¹ (Einasto), 3.6×10²¹ (Burkert); |l|,|b| < 30°: 4.5×10²². Hence Φ₁₀° = 6.0×10⁻⁵ (f₂/τ_γ/10⁻²² s⁻¹)(TeV/m_χ) ph cm⁻² s⁻¹, Φ₃₀°box = 3.6×10⁻⁴, Φ_allsky = 1.6×10⁻³. For a 10° SPI sensitivity of 3×10⁻⁶–3×10⁻⁵ (recalled), f₂/τ_γ < 5×10⁻²⁴–5×10⁻²³ s⁻¹. The redshifted continuum has a sharp edge at E = δ, where its ratio to the CXB (E²dN/dE = 13.5 keV cm⁻² s⁻¹ sr⁻¹ at 300 keV) peaks; ≤ 100 % (10 %) of the CXB gives f₂/τ_γ < 3.3×10⁻²⁰ (3.3×10⁻²¹) s⁻¹ — 650× weaker than the line but profile-independent. Models: Higgsino (τ_γ ~ 0.06 s) and photon-M1/MiDM (66 μs) leave f₂ = 0; the kinetic-mixing dark photon has BR_γ = 0 (the A′–γ insertion ∝ q² vanishes on shell). With a UV dipole, the largest LZ-allowed population (f₂ = 1.2×10⁻³, τ_tot = 7.2×10¹⁶ s) gives Φ₁₀° = 1.0×10⁻² BR_γ, so SPI requires BR_γ < 3×10⁻⁴–3×10⁻³ at δ = 300 keV. A generic photon-only χ₂ with f₂ = 0.42 yields Φ₁₀° = 2.5×10⁻³ at τ_γ = 10²⁰ s — 2.5× the 511 keV bulge — and is excluded for τ_γ < 0.8–8×10²² s (Fig. 1). Signature: FWHM 0.40 keV, a ±0.25 keV solar-motion dipole, unresolved by SPI/COSI; GC/anti-centre contrast 19 (NFW), 5.5 (Burkert); no disk; no known line nearer than ⁷Be 478 keV.

**Caveats.** Every sensitivity is a recalled ×3 bracket; coded-mask sensitivity to ≳ 10° diffuse emission is systematics-limited and needs an instrument-team analysis; the CXB level is uncertain ×2 and "10 % residual" stands in for source modelling; halo parameters move D by ×0.5–1.3; fluxes scale as 1/m_χ (0.26–4 TeV allowed); the exothermic bound is P011/P026's.

**Conclusion.** The MeV sky already limits f₂/τ_γ to ≲ 5×10⁻²³ s⁻¹, complementing LZ's exothermic bound: any LZ-fitting χ₂ needs BR_γ ≲ 10⁻³ at δ = 300 keV, while both LZ models predict no line. COSI (2027) can retest the window with an independent, non-coded-mask response.
