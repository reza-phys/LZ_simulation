# P095: Migdal electrons and bremsstrahlung photons accompanying a 248 keV xenon recoil: extra quanta, their shift in S1–S2, and the −1.5σ band position
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-16
- Primary arXiv category: hep-ph (cross-list physics.ins-det)
- Author profile: atomic/nuclear theory group
- Category (from your taxonomy): NUC (RESP aspects)
- Builds on / responds to: LZ arXiv:2609.02823; P009, P024, P036, P041, P043, P056, P064

**Abstract.** A 248 keV Xe recoil (v_A = 604 km/s, q_e = m_e v_A = 1.03 keV) shakes its own electrons. Two dipole-limit estimates give inner-shell Migdal ionisation probabilities of 1.6×10⁻⁵ (K), 6×10⁻⁴ (L) and 0.6–1.4 % (M) per recoil; nuclear bremsstrahlung above 1 keV occurs in 7×10⁻⁵. Every companion adds ER-like charge and lifts the event in log₁₀S2c by +2.6σ (1 keV) to +14σ (35 keV). The LZ event is 1.5σ charge-poor: wrong sign.

**Question and approach.** How often does the DM kick leave an inner-shell hole (Ibe et al. 2018) or radiate a keV photon (Kouvaris & Pradler 2017), where does the extra ER energy move a 248 keV NR in (S1c, log₁₀S2c), and can it bear on the −1.5σ position (P009, P024)? We evaluate P_f = q_e²|⟨f|Σz|i⟩|² per subshell (A) by the closure sum rule q_e²N⟨z²⟩ with hydrogenic orbitals, Slater charges and the exact continuum share 0.283, and (B) by the oscillator-strength route dP/dE_e = q_e²(df/dω)/(2ω), σ ∝ ω⁻²·⁷. Bremsstrahlung is the sudden-kick dipole spectrum with radiating charge Z_eff(ω) = N(E_B < ω). Companions add Table S3 ER quanta (nestpy) to the P009-scale NR; shifts are in P024's σ = 0.0313 dex.

**Results.** *Companions.* K/L/M ionisation per recoil: 1.6×10⁻⁵, (4.5–7.6)×10⁻⁴, (0.64–1.43)×10⁻² (routes A/B within ×2.2; K matches the Stobbe integral). Mean E_ER = E_e + E_B = 55, 8.0, 1.26 keV; Auger electrons travel 0.02–9 μm, K X-rays 0.5 mm, far below the 3.1 mm S2 diffusion: one merged S2. N/O shells (q_e r = 0.4–1.3, beyond the dipole limit) are shaken in every recoil but carry ≈30 eV, 4×10⁻⁴ of the 71–83 keV electronic energy Lindhard/NEST already quench (P064). Cascade Xe–Xe collisions (ħ/τ = 51 eV) are adiabatic for the M shell; only the DM kick (54 keV) is sudden. Bremsstrahlung: P(ω > 1 keV) = 7.2×10⁻⁵, 130× below M-shell Migdal.

*Band shift.* E_ER = 0.1/0.5/1/5/35 keV (N_e,ER = 8.5/38/62/156/633) shifts the NR by +0.39/+1.67/+2.57/+5.6/+14.4σ. E_ER > 0.65 keV leaves ±2σ, > 2.7 keV leaves the ROI (+4.3σ); 39.6 keV gives +15.3σ (P036: +15.8σ). Probability-weighted shift +0.03σ (K+L+M); Migdal lifts 1.1 % of NRs above +2σ (Gaussian tail 2.3 %) and 0.10 % above the ROI ceiling — a 10⁻³ acceptance loss.

*Charge-poor side.* 269 vs 301 electrons (−10.8 %): recombination fluctuation P = 5.7 % (P024); a 2 % position residual alone needs 5.7σ; a lifetime mis-correction needs τ_e = 3.0 ms where 5 ms is assumed (P041); g₂ −3 % gives 0.44σ (P043); ER leakage ≤4×10⁻⁵ (P056).

**Caveats.** Hydrogenic orbitals and a universal continuum share/slope carry a factor-2 uncertainty; binding-energy Z_eff raises route A by ×2–7. Bound excitations to empty levels also create holes: the closure bound is 5 % for M, raising the out-of-band fractions to ≈4 % and 2×10⁻³. Ibe et al.'s tabulated normalisation was not reproduced. The K-shell kick is only marginally sudden. NEST's β model gives no photons below 0.7 keV.

**Conclusion.** Prompt electronic companions of a 248 keV recoil are rare (≈1 % M-shell, 10⁻⁴ bremsstrahlung), always charge-rich, and displace an NR upward by 2–18σ; they cannot produce a charge-poor event and cost ≈10⁻³ in acceptance. The −1.5σ position remains a 6 % recombination fluctuation.
