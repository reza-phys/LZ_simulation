# P038: The efficiency edge: how the S1c < 600 phd boundary shapes inference at 250–270 keV and what an extended ROI would have shown
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-09
- Primary arXiv category: physics.data-an (cross-list hep-ex)
- Author profile: analysis-methods experimentalists
- Category (from your taxonomy): STAT (RESP)
- Builds on / responds to: LZ arXiv:2609.02823 (Data Analysis, Figs. 4/S2/S4, MSSI table, raster scan); P009, P021, P002, P015, P016, P020, P024

**Abstract.** LZ's S1c < 600 phd edge (50 % efficiency at 269.9 keV) was fixed in 2023, before the AmBe calibration and the failed high-energy salt. We rebuild the roll-off with NEST-LZ, move the edge to 700–1200 phd, and propagate it through inelastic-O₁ acceptance, the single-event likelihood and MSSI backgrounds. The δ ≈ 380 keV preference (P021) exists only because the edge hides 97 % of that spectrum; an ROI to 1000 phd would have cost 0.003 NR-band background events.

**Question and approach.** How does inference change (a) if E_true = 262 rather than 248 keV, (b) with S1c < 800 or 1000 phd, (c) in the raster scan over δ? Efficiency: nestpy `GetQuanta` on P009's paper-contour scale (N_q = 11.32 E^1.112), binomial S1 (g1 = 0.110), S2c < 10^4.15; ε_X(E) = 0.955 P(pass). Signal: WimPyDD O₁ isoscalar, 1000 GeV, Baxter halo, full recoil window (to 800 keV), σ_E = 11√(E/248) keV. Statistic: P021's companion-free q0 = 2[ln(f̃/b) − 1 + b/f̃], f̃ = accepted density at E_obs per accepted event, b = 5.7×10⁻⁴/70 keV⁻¹; κ̂ = 1/S_acc. Backgrounds: MSSI interpolated between LZ's WS-ROI and HE-SB (800–1700 phd) counts, 4.7 t FV; NR-band fraction from Fig. S4.

**Results.** *Edge.* E50 = 271.6 keV for 600 phd (Fig. S2: 269.9; rms residual 0.03), 310/349/423/495 keV for 700/800/1000/1200 phd. ε(248) = 0.945, ε(262) = 0.79 (LZ curve 0.71).

*Acceptance and δ.* The 600 phd edge accepts 81/44/12/2.9 % of the δ = 300/350/370/380 keV spectra; 1000 phd accepts ≥ 90 % at every δ. Per ROI event, the best-fit couplings predict 1.06 (350), 6.8 (370), 30 (380 keV) events in 600–1000 phd, which Fig. S4 shows empty. Z(δ) with the 600 edge peaks at 380 keV (3.47σ; P021 3.59σ, agreement ≤ 0.11σ for δ ≤ 385), LR(380/300) = 11.9; with 1000 phd it peaks at 355 keV (2.89σ), Z(380) = 2.33σ, LR(380/300) = 0.48. The event drops from the 60th to the 1.9th percentile of the accepted δ = 380 keV spectrum; κ̂ shrinks ×2.1/7.8/31 at 350/370/380 keV.

*Backgrounds.* MSSI rises steeply with S1c (RFR λ ≈ 130 phd): extending to 800/1000/1200 phd adds 0.006/0.018/0.046 MSSI events, of which 0.0009/0.0027/0.0069 lie within ±2σ of the NR median.

*E_true = 262 keV.* 1/ε = 1.26–1.40 (vs 1.06); δ_max(1 TeV, June) 386.6 → 389.4 keV; with E_obs = 262 the peak moves to 390 keV. P(detected event within 25 keV of the 50 % point) = 1.1 % (δ = 300), 4.8 % (350), 38 % (380); flat spectrum 10 %.

**Caveats.** One-dimensional observed-energy proxy (P021); q0 assumes zero events in the extension (Fig. S4) and cancels its background. MSSI in 600–800 phd is interpolated (×1.8 model spread, ×4 NR-band fraction). nestpy's generator is unseeded (E50 scatter 0.2 keV). One mass and operator; the mechanism is window-, not operator-, specific.

**Conclusion.** The 2023 edge removes the high-energy lobe (peak 343 keV) of every δ ≥ 350 keV spectrum; it manufactures the large-δ preference and inflates reportable couplings up to ×31. A 1000 phd edge (E50 = 423 keV) with an S2c ceiling tracking the NR band costs 0.003 NR-band events.
