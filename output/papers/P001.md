# P001: How much evidence is one event? A Bayesian and frequentist reassessment of the LZ 248 keV recoil
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-03
- Primary arXiv category: physics.data-an (cross-list hep-ex)
- Author profile: astrostatistics / particle-statistics group
- Category (from your taxonomy): STAT
- Builds on / responds to: LZ paper arXiv:2609.02823; P000 dossier

**Abstract.** We convert LZ's single 248 keV event (3.4σ local, 2.6σ global) into Bayes factors and posterior dark-matter (DM) probabilities with a Poisson-in-a-box model (B₁₀ = 1 + E_π[s e⁻ˢ]/b exactly). Against the modelled background alone B₁₀ ≈ 100–500; an Occam penalty for the ~14 effectively distinct spectra reduces it to 7–37, matching the Sellke–Bayarri–Berger cap (≤ 15) from the global p-value. The posterior is governed by the prior odds of DM against unmodelled backgrounds, not by the modelled background.

**Question and approach.** What evidence does one event carry when the modelled background near it is b ≈ 2 × 10⁻⁴ (±2σ of the NR median at S1c > 500 phd; the whole panel holds 0.0106 ± 0.0008), and how does it depend on the signal prior π(s), the prior probability π_DM of DM in this window, and an unknown-background hypothesis U (prior π_U, likelihood L_U)? We use n = 1 Poisson counting with b ∈ {2 × 10⁻⁴, 10⁻³, 0.0106}; priors uniform on [0, s_max] (s_max = 3, 10, 30), log-uniform on [0.01, 30] and Gamma (mean 1 or 2); two- and three-hypothesis posteriors; a mixture Occam factor versus the paper's toy-MC look-elsewhere effect; and projections for further events.

**Results.** *Frequentist.* P(≥1|b) gives one-sided 3.54σ, 3.09σ, 2.31σ; LR_max = e^(b−1)/b = 1840, 368, 35 gives asymptotic 3.88σ, 3.44σ, 2.67σ. The paper's 3.4σ implies an effective single-event background b_eff = 3.4×10⁻⁴ (exact Poisson) to 1.1×10⁻³ (asymptotic), because only ~20–60% of a unit signal falls in a post-hoc box around the event; b ≈ 10⁻³ is LZ-equivalent and 2×10⁻⁴ optimistic.

*Bayes factors.* For uniform s ∈ [0, 10] (E = 0.100), B₁₀ = 501, 101, 10.4. Other priors change B₁₀ by ≤ ×8 (168 to 1483 at b = 2×10⁻⁴); the prior-independent bound is 1 + e⁻¹/b.

*Occam/LEE.* p_global/p_local gives N_eff = 13.9 trials. Equal-weight mixing over N_eff spectra gives B₁₀/N_eff = 36 (b = 2×10⁻⁴) or 7.3 (10⁻³), bracketing the SBB bound −1/(e p ln p) = 14.7 from the 2.6σ global p.

*Posteriors.* Versus modelled background: P(DM) = 0.33/0.84/0.96 for π_DM = 0.001/0.01/0.05 at b = 2×10⁻⁴; 0.09/0.51/0.84 at 10⁻³. With U, as b → 0, P(DM) → π_DM E/(π_DM E + π_U L_U): the modelled background drops out and P(DM) > ½ requires π_DM > 10 π_U L_U. For π_DM = 0.01, (π_U, L_U) = (0.1, 0.1): P(DM) = 0.09, P(U) = 0.90; for π_DM = 0.05, P(DM) = 0.33 (Fig. 1).

*Next equal exposure* (π_DM = 0.01, π_U = 0.1): 0/1/2 further events give P(DM) = 0.03/0.22/0.80.

**Caveats.** The box model assumes unit signal efficiency in a region drawn around the event; the true LZ likelihood uses 2D PDFs and spectra, which we mimic only through b_eff. L_U and π_U are judgemental; counting cannot separate DM from an unknown background of similar rate. Wilks' asymptotics overstate Z by ~0.3σ at these b, so all significances are indicative.

**Conclusion.** One event is very strong evidence against the *modelled* background (B₁₀ ~ 10²–10³) but only positive-to-strong evidence for DM once the model space (÷14) and unmodelled backgrounds are admitted; P(DM) exceeds ½ only if π_DM > 10 π_U L_U.
