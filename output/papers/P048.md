# P048: Chargino lifetimes, disappearing tracks and the road to a thermal Higgsino: HL-LHC, FCC-hh and a muon collider after LZ
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-10
- Primary arXiv category: hep-ph
- Author profile: future-collider phenomenologists
- Category (from your taxonomy): COMP
- Builds on / responds to: LZ arXiv:2609.02823; P007 (fit points), P014 (Δm±, cτ; competitor), P037 (Y = 1 triplet), P018/P034 (δ systematics)

**Abstract.** The LZ-fitting electroweak doublet has cτ(χ±) = 0.71 cm (P014) and a neutral splitting δ ≈ 366 keV with no collider signature. We compute Drell–Yan yields, chargino βγ_T distributions and tracklet survival at HL-LHC, FCC-hh and a 10 TeV muon collider. HL-LHC reaches 300–475 GeV, FCC-hh 1.3–2 TeV; the muon collider, where βγ = 4.4 is fixed, gives 1 490 tracklets at 1.1 TeV and discovery to 3 TeV. δ is unmeasurable at any collider.

**Question and approach.** Which future machine can produce, see and identify the LZ-fitting doublet, and what would it measure? Hadron cross-sections come from τ-scaling of P014's 13 TeV anchors (reproduced to 0.5 %); muon-collider rates from the tree-level γ/Z formula with the vector-like chargino coupling (massless check 1.129 reproduced). A toy Drell–Yan model (pair mass ∝ M^{−2a−1}β(3−β²)/2, angular law 2 − β²sin²θ) gives βγ_T = p_T/m, the only variable controlling the transverse decay radius; ε_kin = 0.020 is calibrated on the ATLAS wino exclusion. Reach uses recalled fake backgrounds (30/100/3 events) with ×/÷3 cross-section bands.

**Results.** Produced pairs at 1.1 TeV: 2.3×10³ (HL-LHC), 1.8×10⁶ (FCC-hh, σ_cn = 41 fb, band 14–120) and 1.7×10⁴ at 10 TeV (χ⁺χ⁻ 1.20 fb, χ₁χ₂ 0.50 fb; the Y = 1 triplet 4.6× more). Median βγ_T is 0.50 (14 TeV) and 0.72 (100 TeV), so P(r > 5 cm) = 1.1×10⁻³ and 9.9×10⁻³, while the muon collider's βγ = 4.4 gives 0.125.

Muon-collider mono-photon is 8 % of the pair rate (0.14 fb) against 3.8 pb of νν̄γ: S/√B ≈ 0.2 inclusively. The muon collider also measures σ_prod (doublet vs Y = 1 triplet at 7.7σ for 20 % precision) and cτ to 2.6 %, hence Δm± to 3 MeV. On δ: χ₂ → χ₁γ gives a 63 keV–2.1 MeV photon (γ = 3) after 5×10⁷ m — 0.16 in-detector decays in the whole FCC-hh programme; δ enters Δm± at (0.1–2.1)×10⁻³, so a ±10 MeV splitting measurement bounds δ < 4.7–104 GeV, 10⁴–10⁵ times the LZ value; the doublet's W parameter, 1.9×10⁻⁶ at 1.1 TeV, is reachable by FCC-ee only below 278 GeV. Direct detection alone fixes δ: 6.2 keV/√N statistically, ±10–17 keV from the halo tail (P018); a 5 % collider mass moves δ(N=1) by 0.65 keV. Timeline: HL-LHC 2030–41, FCC-ee 2045–65, muon collider 2050s+, FCC-hh 2070s.

**Caveats.** All cross-sections, radii, backgrounds and dates are recalled (flagged in details.md): FCC-hh reach moves ±20 % with either band; ε_kin carries a factor 2; pair p_T from ISR is neglected (conservative); the mono-photon estimate omits shape and polarisation; τ_γ(χ₂) is an O(1) loop estimate.

**Conclusion.** No machine before a 10 TeV muon collider (2050s) or FCC-hh (2070s) can see the LZ-fitting doublet; the muon collider would identify it (m, Y, Δm±) but never δ, which remains a direct-detection observable.
