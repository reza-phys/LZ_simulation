# P047: Isotope by isotope: which xenon nuclei produce a 248 keV recoil for each interaction, and what isotopically modified xenon could reveal
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-10
- Primary arXiv category: nucl-th (cross-list hep-ex)
- Author profile: nuclear theorists and xenon-isotope experimentalists
- Category (from your taxonomy): NUC
- Builds on / responds to: LZ arXiv:2609.02823; P003, P017, P021, P030, P032 (P002, P007 for δ values)

**Abstract.** Two detectors with different xenon isotope mixtures can break the L10/inelastic degeneracy of LZ's lone event. With WimPyDD's per-isotope shell-model responses we decompose the 225–271 keV rate for O1 (elastic; inelastic δ = 300–380 keV), O4, O6, O10 and L10 into isotopes. Spin operators live on ¹²⁹Xe/¹³¹Xe, inelastic scattering near δ_max on ¹³⁴Xe/¹³⁶Xe. In 90 % ¹³⁶Xe-enriched xenon every spin rate falls ×0.106 while the δ = 366 keV rate is unchanged: nine events separate them at 3σ.

**Question and approach.** For each interaction, which isotopes produce a 248 ± 23 keV recoil, and could ¹³⁶Xe-depleted, ¹²⁹Xe-enriched or ¹³⁶Xe-enriched xenon change the window rate by a measurable factor? We run `diff_rate(..., isotopes_list)` at 1 TeV (LZ unit coupling; Baxter halo, 16 June for inelastic) for each isotope, integrate over 225–271 keV and the efficiency-weighted ROI, and re-weight by composition, R′ = Σ R_i (a′_i/a_i)(⟨A⟩/⟨A′⟩). Kinematics: `delta_max_kev(248, 1000, A)` per isotope. Discrimination: exact binomial test on the count fraction in a natural + modified detector pair (equal exposure, normalisation free). ¹²⁴Xe/¹²⁶Xe lack density matrices (≤ 0.2 % effect).

**Results.** *Fractions (window; Fig. 1).* O1 elastic: ¹²⁹ 40 %, ¹³¹ 23 %, ¹³² 20 %, ¹³⁴ 7 %, ¹³⁶ 3 % — the nodes (251–278 keV, P017) enhance ¹²⁹Xe ×1.6 and suppress ¹³⁶Xe ×0.33 relative to A²·abundance. L10 69/31, O4 51/49, O6 40/60, O10 40/60 (¹²⁹/¹³¹), matching P017. Inelastic: ¹³⁴+¹³⁶ carry 12 % (δ = 300), 22 % (366), 58 % (380 keV); odd isotopes 61 → 16 %.

*Kinematics.* δ_max(248 keV, June) = 379/381/383/386/388/393/397 keV for A = 128…136 (mean-A 386.6); ¹³⁶Xe exceeds 50 % of the window rate for δ ≥ 384 keV.

Full ROI (¹³⁶-enriched): δ = 366/380 rise 2.31/4.55. For spin operators ρ = 0.1065 is exactly independent of the ¹²⁹/¹³¹ split. Double ratio D(δ=366/L10) = 9.4 (380: 29); nine window events in total — 12.8 t·yr per detector at LZ's rate — reject L10 at 3σ if δ = 366 keV is true (14 if L10 is true); five at δ = 380 keV; 49 to separate inelastic from elastic O1; ¹²⁹-enriched needs 54, ¹³⁶-depleted ~4000. Isotopes cannot tell spin operators apart (D = 1).

**Caveats.** Stock DMFormFactor-v6 one-body responses; per-isotope SI window shares are ±10–20 % (shell vs Helm 0.83–1.11), moving D(366/L10) to 7.8–11; ±15 % single-isotope spin variations shift f₁₂₉ by ±0.035 (matters only for ¹²⁹-enrichment). Two-body currents (recalled) act differently on ¹²⁹Xe and ¹³¹Xe but cancel for the ¹³⁶-enriched ratio. Efficiency and windows are true-energy proxies; the exposures assume LZ's rate.

**Conclusion.** nEXO-grade ¹³⁶Xe-enriched xenon in a dual-phase TPC is a clean discriminant: spin-dependent (L10/O6/O10) rates drop ninefold, Higgsino-like inelastic rates do not (rising 2–5× in the full ROI). Nine events across a natural/enriched pair decide; depleted or ¹²⁹Xe-enriched xenon does not.
