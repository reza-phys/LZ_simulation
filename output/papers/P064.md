# P064: Nuclear-recoil quenching at 250 keV: Lindhard theory, the NEST power law and the energy-scale systematic of the LZ event
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-12
- Primary arXiv category: physics.ins-det (cross-list nucl-th)
- Author profile: nuclear-recoil quenching theorists
- Category (from your taxonomy): NUC (RESP)
- Builds on / responds to: LZ arXiv:2609.02823 (Table S5, detector-response supplement); P009, P024, P043, P038

**Abstract.** LZ's energy scale N_q = 11.2 E^1.1 encodes a quenching factor L = N_q W/E. Lindhard theory for xenon gives L rising as E^0.16 (β_eff = 1.15–1.19), steeper than β = 1.1. Anchored to the D-D calibration at 74.7 keV, any Lindhard curve predicts 7–9 % more quanta at 248 keV — P009's contour offset — and 244–249 keV for the event. LZ's ± 23 keV systematic equals a slope uncertainty δβ ≈ 0.085; the p(E) break is Thomas–Imel recombination saturation, not new stopping-power physics.

**Question and approach.** (a) What does Lindhard predict for L(E) in Xe at 50–300 keV, and how does β = 1.1 compare? (b) Where do Table S5 and P009's contour scale (11.32 E^1.112) sit relative to Lindhard? (c) Which slope or k uncertainty explains the ± 23 keV systematic (P043: ≈ 10 % light yield)? (d) How do the p(E) break and Thomas–Imel recombination shape the partition and band? We evaluate L = k g(ε)/(1 + k g(ε)) (k = 0.166 ± 20 %; recalled fit values 0.139/0.174), the analytic NEST v2 yields (validated against nestpy to 0.11 %), the Thomas–Imel box model with NEST's ς = 0.0408, and invert N_q = 5179.

**Results.** *Lindhard vs power law.* L(k = 0.166) = 0.258/0.274/0.334/0.345 at 50/74.7/248/300 keV, d ln L/d ln E = 0.163. Table S5 corresponds to an energy-dependent k = 0.136 (74.7 keV) → 0.120 (248 keV), the contour scale to 0.147 → 0.133. Unanchored, the event is 265 (Table S5), 247 (contour), 241 (k = 0.139), 218 (k = 0.166) or 212 keV (k = 0.174).

*Anchored systematics.* With N_q fixed at 74.7 keV, β′ = 1.05/1.10/1.15/1.20 gives 281/265/251/238 keV; d ln E/dβ′ = −1.09. Lindhard k = 0.133–0.199 anchored gives 244–249 keV (± 2.5 keV) and N_q(248)/N_q,S5 = 1.07–1.09 (1.080 at k = 0.166; P009: 1.077). The paper's ± 23 keV corresponds to δβ′ = 0.085 and P043's 10.4 % scale to 0.087 — the power-law-vs-Lindhard slope difference.

*Partition.* At 248 keV the break lowers N_e from 378 to 301, shifting log₁₀N_e by 0.0995 dex = 3.2σ (P024's 1.5σ → 4.7σ; MC 5.0σ) while changing N_ph by 1.7 %. The Thomas–Imel box model with NEST's ς and N_ex/N_i = 1.40 gives N_e = 301.0 (248 keV) and 320.7 (300 keV) against LZ's 300.8 and 319.9; N_ex/N_i = 1 gives 318. Band slopes d log N_e/d log N_ph: 0.286 (break), 0.28–0.30 (TI), 0.45 (no break).

*AmBe degeneracy.* Rescaling N_q by 1.077 shifts the band 0.010 dex at 540 phd, but refitting (a, b) → (0.0306, 0.0137) restores it to 0.0015 dex rms while the event moves 265 → 247 keV. The AmBe points end at S1c ≈ 600 phd; the 333 keV endpoint (696–904 phd) is unobserved.

**Caveats.** Lindhard's g(ε) is a parametrisation with 10–20 % theoretical spread; k = 0.139/0.174 are recalled (likely/uncertain). Aprile 2006 covered 10–57 keV, so a 75 keV stopping-power kink is untestable there. N_ex/N_i = 1.40 is nestpy's; measurements suggest ≈ 1.

**Conclusion.** LZ's 248 keV is the D-D-anchored Lindhard answer; the printed β = 1.1 extrapolation (265 keV) is the outlier. The ± 23 keV systematic is the unmeasured 75–250 keV yield slope, which AmBe cannot fix because the p(E) parameters absorb any light-yield rescaling. The break is recombination saturation, robust physics.
