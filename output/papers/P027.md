# P027: Bayesian model comparison across 293 spectra given one event: which model classes carry the posterior?
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-08
- Primary arXiv category: physics.data-an (cross-list hep-ph)
- Author profile: Bayesian statisticians in astroparticle physics
- Category (from your taxonomy): STAT
- Builds on / responds to: LZ paper arXiv:2609.02823 (Tables S6–S7, LEE supplement); P001, P002, P003, P008, P016, P020

**Abstract.** We put a prior over LZ's model space (298 distinguishable spectra: elastic O1, O3–O15, L10; inelastic O1/O4 at 400–4000 GeV) and marginalise coupling and model against P016's joint likelihood (one event at b_H = 5.7 × 10⁻⁴, low-energy null). The model-marginalised Bayes factor is B_DM = 16 (uniform over spectra) to 29 (class priors), a Bayesian trials factor of 6–10 relative to the best model's 171. The posterior is diffuse (65–99 effective models), 58–85 % inelastic, < 10⁻⁴ SI-like.

**Question and approach.** What is the posterior over models, how concentrated is it, and what is the model-marginalised DM-versus-background Bayes factor — the Bayesian analogue of the LEE? Per spectrum, L/L₀ = e^{−s}(1 + ρs/b_H) W: s = expected 200–270 keV events, W = marginal likelihood ratio of the low-energy NR-band bins and the empty 125–200 keV bin, ρ = optional in-window shape factor (pdf at 248 keV, 23 keV resolution). Coupling priors: log-uniform s ∈ [10⁻³, 30], uniform [0, 10], Jeffreys, or on the total ROI signal μ. Model priors: uniform over spectra, over four classes, physics-weighted (inelastic = elastic), raw models, heavy-only. δ is also treated continuously.

**Results.** *Per model* (log-uniform s): companion-free 171; O1^v/O4^s 1000 GeV δ = 350 keV 169/159; L10 71, O6 54, O10 30, O1^s 0.06; all 123 spectra with m ≤ 50 GeV have B = 0. P016's flat-prior values are reproduced except L10 (30.6, not 44.8; true N_M = 1.16, not 0.70).

*Marginal Bayes factor* (Fig. 2). Uniform over 298 spectra: B_DM = 16.4 (log-uniform), 6.6 (uniform), 13.8 (Jeffreys), 15.9 (with ρ); class priors 28.7; raw 469 models 13.7. B_max/B_DM = 10.3 matches P008's N_eff = 12.2 and P001's B/N_eff = 12.7–14.4; class priors halve it.

*Posterior.* No model exceeds 3.4 % (5.5 % with class priors); O1^v and O4^s at δ = 300–350 keV lead, L10 is 14th (1.5 %). 50 % / 90 % of the mass needs 15–29 / 64–90 models; exp(entropy) = 65–99. Class masses (uniform / class priors): inelastic 0.58 / 0.85 (prior 0.19 / 0.50), δ ≥ 300 keV 0.32 / 0.46, q²-suppressed spin 0.30 / 0.11, elastic O1 < 10⁻⁴.

*Continuous δ.* The δ-averaged B (uniform to μv²_max/2) is 15–72 versus 10–58 on LZ's grid, and the tuning penalty B_max/B̄ is 2.4–10. The 266 keV node (ρ = 0.36–0.57 at δ ≤ 340 keV) confines the O1^s δ-posterior to 318–379 keV; O1^v/O4^s are broad.

*Prediction* (6.76 t·yr): model average 0.92 events, P(0) = 0.58, P(≥2) = 0.20.

**Caveats.** b_H is anchored to LZ's 3.4σ; all B scale as 1/b_H. Our operator basis is not LZ's Lagrangian list (12 of 40 map directly), so class fractions carry ±0.2 systematic. Low-energy data are P016's 1D projection of Fig. 5; ρ assumes a flat in-window background. Annual-average halo; June raises δ ceilings by 12–16 keV.

**Conclusion.** Marginalising 298 spectra yields B_DM ≈ 16–29: positive-to-strong evidence against the modelled background, with an Occam factor equal to the frequentist N_eff. The posterior spreads over ~70–100 models, dominated by inelastic O1^v/O4 at δ ≥ 250 keV and q²-suppressed spin operators; SI-like couplings are excluded.
