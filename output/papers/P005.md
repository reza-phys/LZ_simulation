# P005: Would XENONnT and PandaX-4T have seen it? Expected high-energy counts in their published regions of interest
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-03
- Primary arXiv category: hep-ph (cross-list hep-ex)
- Author profile: phenomenologists who recast direct-detection results
- Category (from your taxonomy): XEXP
- Builds on / responds to: LZ paper (arXiv:2609.02823, Table I, Figs. 1, S2, Table S7); dossier §6 item 5; anticipates P003 (absolute couplings)

**Abstract.** LZ fits its 248 keV event with 1.0 (+1.4, −0.7) signal events in 2.84 t·yr. Because XENONnT (3.1 t·yr) and PandaX-4T (1.54 t·yr) share the xenon target, their expected counts follow from LZ's normalisation and their efficiencies alone. Folding WimPyDD spectra for LZ's best-fit models into candidate ROIs, we find 0–0.23 expected events in both experiments combined for ROIs ending at ≤100 keV, versus 1.5 (P(0) = 22%) with LZ-like 270 keV ROIs. Their 2025 null results are uninformative.

**Question and approach.** Should the other tonne-scale xenon experiments already have registered LZ's putative signal? For natural xenon and a common halo model, dR/dE_R per tonne-year is model-dependent but detector-independent; only exposure and ε(E_R) differ. We compute spectra with WimPyDD (LZ's code) for inelastic isoscalar O1 (m = 1000 GeV, δ = 250/300/350 keV) and the L10 magnetic-dipole interaction, normalise each to 1.0 LZ event through a smooth model of LZ's efficiency (0.96 plateau, 50% at 5.4 and 269.9 keV), and fold with ε = 0.9 inside ROIs ending at 55, 70, 100, 150 keV or with LZ's 270 keV roll-off. The published edges are recalled only approximately (XENONnT ≈60 keV, PandaX-4T ≲100–120 keV; both uncertain), hence the scan.

**Results.** L10 is implemented as the dipole–dipole reduction 4(q²/m_M²)O4 − 4(m_N²/m_M²)O6 (Anand et al.), whose longitudinal Σ'' response cancels identically; the WimPyDD spectrum reproduces a colour digitisation of LZ Fig. 1 (peaks 21/196 vs 25/202 keV; RMS shape deviation 19%; fraction below 55 keV 7.1% vs 6.3%). Inelastic spectra start at 55, 91 and 155 keV for δ = 250, 300, 350 keV. Best-fit counts (XENONnT/PandaX-4T; ×0.3–2.4 for LZ's 68% band):

| ROI edge | L10 | δ=250 | δ=300 | δ=350 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 55 keV | 0.07/0.04 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 100 keV | 0.15/0.08 | 0.12/0.06 | 0 | 0 |
| 150 keV | 0.34/0.17 | 0.41/0.20 | 0.20/0.10 | 0 |

With LZ-like 270 keV ROIs every model gives 1.02/0.51, i.e. 1.53 combined, P(0) = 0.22 (0.63–0.025 across LZ's band); for ROIs ending at ≤100 keV the combined expectation is ≤0.23 (P(0) ≥ 0.79), and δ ≥ 300 keV gives zero below 91 keV. The 7.48 t·yr xenon world predicts 2.6 events; exactly one has probability 0.19. A 270 keV analysis needs 7.0 (16) t·yr for a 90% chance of ≥1 (≥3) events at best fit, 23 (54) t·yr at the lower band edge.

**Caveats.** The other experiments' ROI edges and in-ROI efficiencies are recalled or assumed (0.9 plateau; counts scale linearly), and their ROIs are defined in cS1 at different drift fields, so keV edges are approximate. WimPyDD's L10 shape deviates locally by up to 40% from LZ Fig. 1 (nuclear inputs); both versions are tabulated and agree to 15% in every count. Time-averaged halo; no backgrounds of the other experiments modelled.

**Conclusion.** The XENONnT and PandaX-4T 2025 results neither support nor constrain LZ's high-energy interpretation: LZ's best-fit models predict ≤0.2 events in their ROIs. Extending the analysis of their existing 4.6 t·yr to 270 keV would expect ≈1.5 events and is the cheapest independent test.
