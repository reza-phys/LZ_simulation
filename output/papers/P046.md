# P046: A multi-target confirmation programme for a 300–390 keV inelastic splitting: xenon, iodine and tungsten as a δ-meter
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-10
- Primary arXiv category: hep-ph (cross-list hep-ex)
- Author profile: experiment-design phenomenologists
- Category (from your taxonomy): XEXP / PROJ
- Builds on / responds to: LZ arXiv:2609.02823; P015 (blindness map, W/Xe ratios), P005/P035 (xenon world), P028 (NaI), P018 (halo tail), P021/P038 (δ preference and ROI edge), P017 (form factors), P012 (L10), P034

**Abstract.** The coupling cancels in the ratio of counts on two nuclei, so a xenon exposure plus a CaWO₄ detector reads the inelastic splitting directly: W/Xe = 16/82/221/693 per tonne at δ = 300/350/366/380 keV (1 TeV). With 20 t·yr of xenon and 100 kg·yr of CaWO₄, σ_δ = 99/10/3.5/0.8 keV statistically, against a ≈ 15 keV halo/form-factor floor and a 20–50 keV mass degeneracy. The 1.1 MeV tungsten endpoint is unobservable; the L10 dipole is separable with 2–200 kg·yr.

**Question and approach.** How precisely do the W/Xe and I/Xe count ratios measure δ; which windows and backgrounds does each target face; what exposures separate δ = 300 from 380 keV and inelastic DM from L10 at 3σ; can tungsten or iodine see the endpoint E₊(δ)? We factorise WimPyDD's rate exactly into δ-independent per-isotope kernels times the Earth-frame velocity integral (validated to 10⁻⁴ against direct calls; LZ normalisation reproduces P015 to 0.5 %), compute O₁ inelastic (δ = 250–395 keV, 1 keV steps; 0.4/1/4 TeV) and L10 rates on Xe, I, W, Ge in realistic windows, normalise to LZ's one event, and use profiled-coupling multinomial Fisher information, Asimov likelihood ratios, spectral-shape Fisher information and toy MLEs.

**Results.** W(10–1300 keV)/Xe(5–1000 keV) = 16.6/85/230/722; I/Xe falls 0.59 → 0.02; L10 gives a δ-flat W/Xe = 0.05–0.07. *δ-meter* (xenon 20 t·yr; CaWO₄ 0.01/0.1/1 t·yr): σ_δ = 306/99/38 keV at δ = 300, 29/10/5.5 at 350, 8.7/3.5/2.4 at 366, 1.5/0.8/0.7 at 380. *Calibration*: v_esc ±16 km/s → ±13 keV, tungsten form factor ×2 → ±12, m = 4 TeV → −21, 400 GeV → +38 keV. *3σ separations*: 300 vs 380 needs 38 kg·yr CaWO₄ (0.3 kg·yr if δ = 380); 300 vs 350 needs 1.3 t·yr; inelastic 300/350/366/380 vs L10 needs 215/17/2.1/0.09 kg·yr. *Endpoint*: E₊(¹⁸⁶W, June) = 1131 keV at δ = 366 (∓78 keV for v_esc ±16 km/s; 618–1547 keV for 0.4–4 TeV), but 0.39 % of the rate lies above 500 keV, the observed edge (E₉₉ = 410–458 keV) is a Helm node, and the spectrum above 300 keV carries no δ information; the onset-and-lobe shape gives 40 keV/√N; a 700 keV ROI suffices. *Backgrounds*: radiogenic neutrons stop at 220 keV on W; ²⁰⁶Pb recoils at 103 keV.

**Caveats.** O₁ isoscalar, one halo family; tungsten M response is Helm (×2, P015) and its L10 spin response a Gaussian placeholder; 1 % calorimeter resolution and all background inputs recalled; Asimov and Fisher asymptotics at few-event counts; the ratio reads δ only for a fixed mass and halo.

**Conclusion.** A 100 kg·yr CaWO₄ detector (W band to 700 keV) paired with 20 t·yr of xenon read to 1 MeV decides δ ≥ 350 keV to ±10 keV and excludes L10 at > 5σ; δ = 300 keV needs a tonne-year. Endpoints are unmeasurable; germanium is the null control.
