# P013: Neutron origins of a 248 keV single scatter: spectra, multiplicity and what the 2.84 t·yr already exclude
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-05
- Primary arXiv category: hep-ex (cross-list physics.ins-det)
- Author profile: underground-physics background modellers
- Category (from your taxonomy): BKG
- Builds on / responds to: LZ paper (arXiv:2609.02823) Discussion and supplement "Neutrons", Table I, Tables S1–S2; dossier §6.7; complements P004 (wall MSSI) and P010 (ER leakage); uses P003, P009

**Abstract.** A 248 keV xenon recoil needs a neutron above 8.2 MeV. The dominant LZ neutron source, ¹⁹F(α,n) in PTFE, ends at 6.3 MeV; only trace ¹³C/¹⁷O/B reactions, ²³⁸U fission (0.5% above threshold) and muon-induced neutrons qualify. Folding recalled spectra and a diffraction-limited angular distribution through a transport Monte Carlo and LZ's own normalisations (≤0.118 detector-NR single scatters; muon UL 4.6×10⁻⁴), we expect 7×10⁻⁶ neutron single scatters in 200–270 keV, ≤5×10⁻⁴ with every conservative choice stacked.

**Question and approach.** Which neutron sources can give a 248 keV recoil, what companion population accompanies one, and how many 200–270 keV single scatters (SS) do (α,n), spontaneous fission and muon-induced neutrons predict? We compute (α,n) endpoints, n–Xe elastic kinematics (E_R = q²/2M) with a black-disk pattern |2J₁(qR)/qR|², R = 6.35 fm (isotropic as upper bound), Watt (a = 0.988 MeV, b = 2.249 MeV⁻¹) and E⁻¹/E⁻² spectra, and transport 8×10⁶ neutrons through a 72.8×145.6 cm LXe cylinder with recalled cross sections (λ_tot = 14 cm at 10 MeV). An SS is exactly one deposit >3 keV (1–5 keV varied) in the active volume, inside a 4.74 t fiducial cylinder. Normalisations: Table I, the veto partition 0.08/0.87/0.05, the supplement.

**Results.** E_n,min = 8.20 MeV. ¹⁹F(α,n) ends at 5.26/6.31 MeV (U/Th chains), E_R,max = 191 keV: it cannot produce the event. ¹³C (10.6 MeV), ¹⁷O (8.9) and ¹⁰B (9.2) can; their share above 8.2 MeV is ≈(4–6)×10⁻⁴ (adopted f_C = 2.5×10⁻³, scanned 5×10⁻⁴–10⁻²). The diffraction pattern (zero at 194 keV) puts ≈1% (0.4–2% over R) of elastic scatters above 10 MeV into 200–270 keV, versus 1–23% isotropically. Isotropic angles with f_C = 10⁻² give the stacked-conservative case (F = 4×10⁻³, 230 companions, 5×10⁻⁴ events).

|---|---|---|---|
| (α,n), black disk | 5.1×10⁻⁵ | 1.9×10⁴ | 6.0×10⁻⁶; 1.6×10⁻⁴ (≤3 low-E events) |

| muon-induced (E⁻¹/E⁻²) | 1.3–2.0×10⁻³ of all SS | 24–40 | 0.6–1.6×10⁻⁵ |

One (α,n) window event implies 2.0×10⁴ ROI single scatters and 2.1×10⁵ NR-band delayed-veto events (fit UL 1.3; 55 observed). Fission's ν−1 neutrons and ≈7 γ must leave no trace (probability 3.8×10⁻⁴). Muon neutrons, with a hard SS spectrum (5 companions above 200 keV), are limited only by LZ's simulated rate. (n,n′γ) hybrids fail: at S1c = 540 the 39.6/80.2 keV hybrids sit +15σ/+22σ above the NR median (event: −1.8σ); 248+39.6 keV lands outside the ROI.

**Caveats.** Cross sections, (α,n) yields, the ¹³C share, fission multiplicities and the muon spectrum are recalled (×2–3 each); the window lies near a diffraction zero (R sensitivity ×2.5), and sharp-edge models overestimate secondary maxima, so F is conservative. The geometry omits Skin and PTFE. The "≤3 low-energy events" bound is recalled. Unmodelled boron- or beryllium-bearing components are covered only up to f_C = 10⁻². AmBe activation leaves no neutron emitter (details §7).

**Conclusion.** No neutron source can produce the LZ event with appreciable probability: 7×10⁻⁶ expected window events centrally, ≤5×10⁻⁴ with all uncertainties adverse, and any surviving scenario predicts 10²–10⁴ low-energy companions that LZ does not see. Neutrons join wall MSSI (P004) and ER leakage (P010) as excluded conventional origins.
