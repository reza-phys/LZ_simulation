# P036: Could the LZ event be a nuclear excitation? Dark-matter-induced ¹²⁹Xe(39.6 keV) and ¹³¹Xe(80.2 keV) transitions and where they land in S1–S2
- Simulated arXiv date: 2026-09-09
- Primary arXiv category: nucl-th (cross-list hep-ph)
- Author profile: nuclear theorists working on DM–nucleus inelastic transitions
- Category (from your taxonomy): NUC
- Builds on / responds to: LZ arXiv:2609.02823 (Theory, Table S7, Waveform Analysis); P013 ((n,n′γ) hybrids), P019, P017, P009/P024 (band), P010, P003

**Abstract.** Spin-dependent dark matter can excite ¹²⁹Xe (39.58 keV) and ¹³¹Xe (80.19 keV), giving a nuclear recoil plus a prompt electron recoil at one vertex. With LZ-tuned NEST yields and WimPyDD O₄ rates, such hybrids never enter the WIMP ROI (log₁₀S2c > 4.15 at any recoil energy), none reproduces (540.1 phd, 9268 phd) — the 39.6 keV ER alone carries 702 electrons against 269 observed — and a coupling giving one elastic 248 keV event predicts ≈2 (R/0.1) hybrids.

**Question and approach.** (a) Where do NR+E* hybrids lie in (S1c, log₁₀S2c) for E_R = 10–320 keV, and can a low-energy NR plus the de-excitation mimic a 248 keV NR? (b) What excitation-to-elastic SD rate ratio does a heavy WIMP give? (c) Could the event be a lower-energy NR plus a 39.6 keV IC electron? We add quanta at the vertex, S1c = g₁(N_ph,NR+N_ph,ER), S2c = g₂(N_e,NR+N_e,ER), with Table S5 (p(E) break) and Table S3 yields, solve the two equations in (E_NR, E_ER), and compute O₄ spectra for ¹²⁹Xe/¹³¹Xe with WimPyDD using inelastic-DM kinematics δ = E* (exact for a nuclear threshold) times a recalled, bracketed structure-factor ratio R = S_inel/S_el = 0.01–1.

**Results.** *Loci.* The 39.6 keV ER alone gives (247 phd, 4.38), the 80.2 keV ER (495 phd, 4.71); adding an NR moves the point right and slightly up (Fig. 1), so every hybrid has log₁₀S2c > 4.15. At S1c = 540.1 the ¹²⁹Xe* hybrid needs E_NR = 157 keV and lands at 4.520, +15.8σ above the NR median (σ = 0.0313 dex); the ¹³¹Xe* hybrid (E_NR = 34 keV) lands at 4.740 (+22.8σ), on the ER band. Predicted S2c is 3.6–5.9× observed; matching S2c would need N_e,NR = −433/−1201. At fixed S1c the residual grows monotonically with E_ER (+0.022 dex/keV, Fig. 3) and vanishes only at E_ER = −2.7 keV: the event is charge-poor, hybrids are charge-rich.

*Rates (1 TeV, Baxter SHM).* The threshold suppresses the rate by f_kin = 0.427 (¹²⁹Xe*) and 0.255 (¹³¹Xe*). Excitation/elastic = 0.36 R. For the coupling giving one elastic O₄^s event in 200–270 keV, (c₄m_v²)² = 0.021, LZ would hold 0.19/1.9/5.8/19 hybrids for R = 0.01/0.1/0.3/1, all outside the ROI, plus 29.8 elastic 5.4–55 keV recoils (P003: 28). A q→0 check finds |⟨S_n⟩| = 0.247/0.198 in WimPyDD's density matrices (0.75 of one-body values), a ×2 uncertainty on absolute rates only.

*Kinematics.* v_min(248 keV) rises from 341 to 390/437 km/s; excitation needs m_χ ≥ 11.9/26.9 GeV.

**Caveats.** R(q) is recalled (Baudis et al. 2013 computed it) and applied q-independently; the LZ SD limit is recalled (×2). Cross-recombination between the NR and ER clouds could only convert ER electrons to photons and would need ≥0.96 recombination — the same unphysical value P010 excluded. ER yields assume the β model for IC electrons/x-rays (NEST default differs ≤9%). σ_ER ≈ 0.053 dex is approximate.

**Conclusion.** The LZ event is not a nuclear-excitation hybrid: every hybrid carries ≥700 electrons, 2.6× the event's total, and sits 16–23σ above the NR band. Were SD couplings large enough to make the event, a hybrid band 0.2 dex below the ER median would hold ≈2 (R/0.1) events.
